# Institutional exemplars for a defensible Pahlavi research workflow

27 September 2026; `/root/model_choice_review`. Four focused exemplars, using institutional/project sources and primary publications. This is methodological evidence, not a model ranking or an endorsement of a new paid run. No models, corpora or accounts were accessed or downloaded. The existing 24 DEV cases, their 15-whole/nine-constrained assessment, and frozen 40-case PAL-REF remain unchanged.

**Main implication:** a credible ancient-language system should make attested evidence and alternative interpretations inspectable. Its scientific value need not depend on producing one confident translation. These projects support curated text, targeted computational tasks and specialist adjudication; they do not establish a universal training regime or a Pahlavi accuracy guarantee.

## 1. LMU electronic Babylonian Library: vetted readings and efficient witness matching

**Institutional identity.** LMU's [project page](https://www.assyriologie.uni-muenchen.de/forschung/ebl/index.html) identifies Enrique Jiménez and the historical 2018–2024 funding period; this is not an end-date claim for the continuing platform. The [2025 LMU account](https://www.lmu.de/en/about-lmu/structure/central-university-administration/media-relations-and-communications/press-room/press-release/hymn-to-babylon-readable-again-thanks-to-ai-2ae70bee.html) describes subsequent scholarly discoveries. Institutional news is not the evaluation protocol.

**Data practice, verified in the [2024 data paper](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/johd.148?tab=article).** At publication, approximately 25,000 tablets had transliterations, alongside a much larger object catalogue. Another expert reviews each transliteration; a permission/revision system records edits. However, many readings are based on photographs and have not been collated against museum originals. Thus expert review and mechanical format validity remain distinct from final certainty. The [author-maintained parser documentation](https://github.com/ElectronicBabylonianLiterature/transliterated-fragments) makes another useful distinction: the production parser checks a sign database, whereas the released parser checks syntax without that sign list.

**Method and evaluation, verified in [Reconstruction of Cuneiform Literary Texts as Text Matching, LREC-COLING 2024, §§4–6](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.1197.pdf).** This is fragment-to-known-work retrieval, not translation or discovery of unknown meanings. One hundred manuscripts generate 965 usable artificially broken fragments; their source manuscripts are excluded from the comparison corpus. Sign n-gram matching reaches up to 0.94 top-three success, versus 0.14 for bag-of-signs/Jaccard. Crucially, every synthetic fragment is guaranteed a matching chapter; real fragments often have no match, so the authors expect lower real precision. In a practical trial, an expert inspecting the top 300 candidates among approximately 27,000 fragments found three previously unidentified manuscripts. This is verified scholarly discovery supported by retrieval, not a measured three-in-300 classifier precision. Most methods ran on an 8 GB laptop; expensive alignment was slower, and more elaborate weighting added little overall precision.

**Transfer to us—inference.** Use stable witness IDs and exact source spans, including corrections and doubts, before deriving lexical supervision. Keep a simple matching baseline for locating attestations. A high overlap score can prioritize reading; it cannot establish that the proposed sense fits the new context. Preserve a genuine “no adequate attestation” outcome, rather than force three examples for every query. This does not retrospectively alter the frozen evidence package.

## 2. Ithaca: task-specific restoration, interpretable alternatives and limited expert baselines

**Identity and evidence.** The [2022 Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04448-z), including Methods, was inspected. Its affiliations place Yannis Assael at DeepMind, Thea Sommerschield at Ca' Foscari/Harvard, and Jonathan Prag at Oxford; calling the entire project solely Oxford would be inaccurate.

Ithaca restores characters and predicts region/date for Greek inscriptions; it does not translate Greek into a modern language. I.PHI contains 78,608 inscriptions after removing 9,441 duplicates. Test and validation use IDs ending in 3 and 4, rather than a held-out-work design. Editorial supplements are retained. Restoration evaluation masks 1–10 known characters and reports length-macro-averaged error/top-k accuracy. The human restoration comparison involved two advanced graduate researchers, explicitly not established specialists. Their top-one restoration accuracy increased from about 25% to 72% with assistance. Disputed Athenian dating cases were separately removed before training. Date performance measures distance to an editorial interval, not exact-year recovery.

**Transfer to us—inference.** Score restoration separately from translation. Artificial damage cannot validate decipherment. Preserve our work-level holdouts. Any reader/model/assisted-reader comparison must document qualifications and information access.

## 3. Aeneas: parallels as research support, with an explicit editorial-bias problem

**Institutional identity.** [Oxford's July 2025 project account](https://classics.web.ox.ac.uk/node/4577076) describes co-leads Assael and Sommerschield, Google DeepMind/Nottingham development, and Warwick/Oxford/Athens collaborators. This is a historical project attribution. [Durham's current staff profile](https://dur.ac.uk/staff/thea-sommerschield/) states that Sommerschield joined Durham in September 2025. It should not be retroactively substituted for every publication affiliation.

**Verified primary evidence.** The [2025 Nature article, readable full text in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408360/), covers Latin restoration, attribution and contextual retrieval. LED contains 176,861 inscriptions/16 million characters; only about 5% have images. ID-based and greater-than-90%-similarity deduplication precede last-digit train/validation/test partitioning. Twenty-three epigraphers assessed 60 test inscriptions; assistance separated retrieved parallels from parallels plus predictions. Restoration CER changed from 39.0% alone to 33.9% with parallels and 21.4% with predictions. “Useful research starting point” agreement was 75% and 90%, respectively—not translation accuracy. Assisted dating remained slightly behind the model alone. Source editions retain conjectural restorations; the authors acknowledge circularity and missing editorial provenance. An ablation excluding conjectures performed worse by under 5% in its evaluation, which does not establish that those conjectures are historically true. Scarce regions/periods perform worse, and image saliency was often hard for experts to interpret.

**Transfer to us—inference.** Separate attestation, editorial supplement and model hypothesis. Preserve annotations and justified alternatives. Measure evidence usefulness, translation correctness and perceived confidence separately. In future human studies, record unaided answers before assistance and retain subsequent changes and errors.

## 4. Chicago RSTI/OCHRE: source-linked lexicography before automatic generalization

**Institutional identity.** The [OCHRE project page](https://voices.uchicago.edu/ochre/project/rsti/) names Miller Prosser and Dennis Pardee as co-directors. The [current Chicago CORPUS directory](https://voices.uchicago.edu/corpus/projects/) associates Prosser with the Forum for Digital Culture and Pardee with ISAC/Middle Eastern Studies. Older publications say Oriental Institute; those labels describe their period, not a separate project.

**Concrete workflow.** Prosser's [2019 project account of parsing](https://voices.uchicago.edu/rsti/2019/02/14/parsing/) describes building Akkadian/Ugaritic glossaries from words encountered in texts, assigning controlled parse properties, and guiding researchers through unidentified forms. Existing attestations produce possible matches; ambiguous alphabetic forms still require a researcher to resolve grammatical interpretation. This is curated lexicography and assisted annotation, not demonstrated autonomous translation. The [2018 scholarly chapter](https://brill.com/display/book/edcoll/9789004375086/B9789004375086_012.pdf) situates the workflow in reliable digital editions integrated with archaeology. Its publisher-indexed opening was accessible, but full-PDF retrieval failed; detailed workflow claims here rely on the author's institutional account, not an uninspected chapter. No controlled accuracy study, TRAIN/TEST split or verified current corpus count was established from these sources. The project page's dynamic item total was not available as a usable number.

**Transfer to us—inference.** Start with attested word-in-context records, not an invented dictionary built from model answers. A useful record links the exact surface form and location to candidate lemma/sense, grammatical evidence, translation span, source edition and adjudication status. Mechanical suggestions can reduce repetitive work without silently choosing among genuinely ambiguous readings. Our first implementation need only be a small existing-format annotation packet; this evidence does not justify adopting a new database platform.

## What changes in our scientific design—and what does not

| User's intended level | Transferable research practice | Claim that remains unsupported |
|---|---|---|
| Attested isolated words | Trace a sense to cited occurrences; preserve homographs, disputed readings and contextual restrictions; independently check mappings. | Whole-passage parallelism alone provides reliable word-level gold. |
| New combinations of known words | Test semantic roles, negation and morphology separately from lexical recall; show only admissible TRAIN witnesses as assistance; keep plain controls. | Recovering the right nouns demonstrates correct composition. |
| New inscriptions with uncertain words | Preserve transcription/reading alternatives, document provenance and related witnesses; present hypotheses for specialist adjudication and allow insufficient-evidence outcomes. | A fluent translation or restoration score certifies a new decipherment. |

The smallest useful next step suggested by these exemplars is **an evidence packet and an explicit error model**, not another large architecture. Reuse qualified TRAIN provenance to establish whether a small set of lexical/compositional assertions can be responsibly supervised. State which assertions come from a physical reading, an editor or a model. A specialist can then accept, qualify or reject each assertion. AI review is useful triage, but not an interchangeable substitute for the philological expertise used in these projects.

For any later experiment, freeze the claim, source partition and information supplied before generation. Exact duplicate removal is insufficient when variant editions, quotations or parallel witnesses cross partitions. Maintain the current work-level exclusions and distinguish familiarity with a known work from generalization to a new one. Preserve alternative valid answers and reviewer disagreement; report critical errors by work as well as aggregate acceptance. Do not turn model probabilities or saliency into calibrated confidence or causal explanations without a separate validation.

This review does not replace PAL-REF, add a new benchmark, change the 24 DEV cases, alter the separate denominators, select a challenger or admit training. Restoration/retrieval/dating precedents support hypotheses about a research assistant; translation and uncertainty claims still require our own appropriate evidence. No institution's result establishes universal superiority or affordable reproduction on our hardware.
