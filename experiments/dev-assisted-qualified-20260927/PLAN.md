## Scientific question and present evidence

The target has three distinct levels: (1) an attested word's context-appropriate meaning, including legitimate alternatives; (2) the meaning of an unfamiliar combination of known words, including roles, negation and morphology; (3) evidence-supported hypotheses about an uncertain reading or unknown meaning. A fluent sentence is insufficient at every level. Level 3 requires corroboration beyond the generating model; it is not certified by a high translation score. Our executed experiments use scholarly transcription, not photographs or direct native-script recognition.

The existing cleaned-data comparison found only small aggregate gains with losses in individual works. It does not identify the cause: examples, token exposure and update count changed together. Roughly 49% of qualified examples come from one work, so an overall score alone can hide narrow specialization. More training, a larger model, raw-text prediction and more fluent Persian are competing hypotheses, not established remedies.

The [local inventory](UNLABELED-DATA-INVENTORY.md) finds 2,237 qualified paired rows with 37,492 source whitespace terms. Of 4,507 raw Parsig rows, only 62 lack both translation fields; these contain 260 terms and are short chapter headings, not a new running-text corpus. None is in frozen TRAIN. Removing targets from paired data provides no new context. This does not prove continued pretraining is useless, but it removes the assumed resource advantage. Other archives and newly identified institutional collections remain uncounted and unqualified for training.

## Learning regimes: decisions and prerequisites

| Approach | Project decision | Evidence needed before training |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supervised translation fine-tuning (current LoRA) | Preserve as baseline. Do not repeat the same recipe without a diagnosed hypothesis. | Attested parallel examples with original qualifications and work-level provenance. |
| Translation plus supervised lexical/grammatical tasks | Preferred next **training hypothesis**, conditional on reliable labels. | Source spans, context-specific senses/alternatives, morphology and construction labels supported by scholarship; independent review, not guessed alignments from whole translations. |
| Continued self-supervised pretraining, then the same translation training | Conditional candidate: TITUS adds a real source reservoir, but its clean eligible size is not established. The first111-page structural probe yields4,665 source terms, not the raw10,484-page-text terms. | Unique works/contexts, representation and language checks, rights, exclusion of held-out sources and parallel witnesses; evaluate translation and retention as well as source loss. |
| Backtranslation or forward pseudo-labeling | Defer. The reverse translator and teacher are unvalidated. | Independent checking of synthetic pairs; agreement with itself or a round trip is not semantic evidence. |
| Preference optimization or reinforcement learning | Defer until preferences/rewards have target-language validation. | Reliable contrasting translations and independently checked rankings; do not train to the same uncalibrated AI judge used to claim success. |
| Retrieved attested evidence at inference | Worth the controlled comparison below; it is not a learning regime. | Split-safe, relevant witnesses with qualifications. Measure harms as well as gains. |

LoRA/QLoRA are parameter-update and numerical techniques, not alternatives to supervised or self-supervised learning. The [five-study regime review](RESEARCH-REGIMES-UPDATE.md) covers positive and negative controlled results: mixed objectives are not automatically better. Historical-language MITRA is useful precedent for mixed pretraining followed by instruction training, but its billions of tokens and additional bilingual supervision do not establish feasibility or causality for our small corpus.

## Institutional research and what can transfer

This is a targeted primary-source evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a meta-analysis. Search routes covered direct Middle Persian projects (MPCD, Invisible East, MIRTEXT/Turfanforschung, CAB, CII), ancient-language computational projects (eBL, Ithaca/Aeneas, MIT decipherment), and controlled low-resource translation studies. We include a project when its institutional provenance and actual task are identifiable and its method or data could address one of the three target levels. Project descriptions establish resources/workflow; papers establish experimental claims. Different tasks, corpora and metrics are not pooled or ranked by headline percentages. Exact sources and access/reuse limitations are in the linked reports.

- **MPCD, Bochum/FU Berlin/Cologne:** manuscript-based transcription, grammar and contextual semantic annotation provide the closest workflow analogue. FU Berlin documents links between Pahlavi Zand words and their Avestan sources. This suggests retaining separate evidence layers and using reviewed senses/constructions; it is not evidence that a ready Pahlavi neural translator exists. [Institutional description](https://www.geschkult.fu-berlin.de/en/e/iranistik/forschung/MPCD/index.html), [annotation methodology](https://www.mpcorpus.org/methodology/).
- **Oxford Invisible East:** documentary Middle Persian can broaden the kinds of text represented. Its citation policy permits use with appropriate editorial/source attribution, but this is not a blanket assertion about every underlying image or edition. English scholarly translations may help verification; do not silently turn machine-translated Persian derivatives into gold or change our output language to simplify ingestion. [Corpus](https://www.invisible-east.org/corpus/?filter_fk_collection=21), [reuse/citation policy](https://www.invisible-east.org/about/cite/).
- **LMU electronic Babylonian Library:** independently reviewed readings and witness matching support an evidence-first workflow. Its strong synthetic fragment-retrieval result assumes a matching chapter exists; real queries need an insufficient-evidence outcome. [Text-matching study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.1197.pdf).
- **Ithaca/Aeneas collaborators:** restoration and contextual-parallel studies motivate assistance with alternatives and separate human/model evaluations. Aeneas's 23-epigrapher study concerns 60 Latin inscriptions, not Pahlavi translation; perceived usefulness and confidence must not become our accuracy measure. [Aeneas paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408360/).
- **Chicago RSTI/OCHRE:** its attestation-linked glossary workflow leaves ambiguous grammatical parses to scholarly judgment. Reuse this principle in a small annotation packet; no new database platform is necessary. [Author's parsing workflow](https://voices.uchicago.edu/rsti/2019/02/14/parsing/).
- **MIT CSAIL and collaborators:** the 2019 minimum-cost-flow model matches cognates using linguistic constraints and related-language vocabulary; its Linear B experiment retained 919 known pairs after excluding uncertain translations. The 2021 follow-up combines segmentation with phonetic priors; Gothic results use top-10 stem matching, and Iberian evidence includes previously identified personal names. These support a future constrained candidate-ranking study, not unrestricted translation of unknown inscriptions. First validate any Pahlavi correspondence rules against attested forms; keep non-cognates and counterexamples. Do not copy phonetic assumptions onto orthographic forms without checking the transcription layer. [2019 paper](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1303.pdf), [2021 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.5.pdf).
- **Johns Hopkins' unsupervised-MT stress test:** domain mismatch and unstable dictionary induction caused severe failures, including very poor real low-resource results. That older pipeline does not settle modern LLM adaptation, but it is a counterexample to assuming unlabeled data automatically solve limited supervision. [Controlled study](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wmt-1.68.pdf).

The [Iranian-institutions review](RESEARCH-INSTITUTIONS-IRANIAN.md) and [ancient-language methods review](RESEARCH-INSTITUTIONS-METHODS.md) separately document eBL, Ithaca/Aeneas and further corpus projects, including human-review practices, synthetic-test limitations and institutional attribution. Restoration, fragment retrieval and dating do not measure translation. Public availability alone does not imply an admitted training license or an uncontaminated test collection.
