# Historical-language model reassessment — 2026-09-27

**Decision: no documented replacement is presently justified over the cloud-tested `google/gemma-4-31B-it` for Pahlavi transcription → Persian.** This is an evidence gap, not a claim that Gemma is intrinsically best. Two released alternatives justify a small comparative experiment after runtime/budget readiness; neither justifies spending the remaining budget on another training run before comparison.

Scope: primary papers and publisher/model-author cards; no inference, weights download, installation, credentials, cloud jobs, or repository code changes. Read the September 25 research and `experiments/retrain-qualified-20260927/STRATEGY-EVIDENCE-INVENTORY.md` first. The relevant project input is scholarly Latin transcription, not an undeciphered script or tablet image. The qualified 2,237 training rows and existing provisional evaluation are local project evidence, not expert-certified ground truth.

## Two credible comparison candidates

| Candidate | Verified documentation and access | What the evidence does and does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **`buddhist-nlp/mitra-qwen35-translate`** | Public Qwen3.5-9B derivative, Apache-2.0. Author card reports approximately 22B domain-pretraining tokens plus curated translation instruction tuning and preference optimization. Inputs are Buddhist-language text, including romanized Tibetan and Pāli examples; sources are Sanskrit, Pāli, Classical Tibetan and Classical/Buddhist Chinese. Named targets include English, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, German and French. Pahlavi and Persian are not explicitly supported. Uses its own prompt template with thinking disabled. | The card reports 500 randomized pairwise comparisons against Claude Opus 4, judged by two LLMs. Overall preference is 64%/52.6%; Pāli→English is 52%/44%. Its blanket claim of winning or tying every pair under both judges is therefore unsupported by its own table. Rare transliteration repetition and weaker very-low-resource directions remain. No Pahlavi superiority, Persian fidelity, expert validation of this checkpoint, or calibrated uncertainty was demonstrated. **Best historical-language comparator, not a validated replacement.** [Author model card](https://huggingface.co/buddhist-nlp/mitra-qwen35-translate). |
| **`tencent/Hy-MT2-7B`** | Public Apache-2.0 translation specialist; the family calls this model 7B, while Hugging Face's tensor summary rounds it to 8B. Modern Persian appears explicitly in its language list; Pahlavi does not. Text/chat inputs include translation, terminology and contextual instructions. BF16 weights, quantized variants and training recipes are published. | A credible test of Persian output and explicit terminology adherence, with no documented Pahlavi reading competence. Its card's language-count labels are inconsistent, so use the actual language list. GGUF deployment notes refer to an STQ kernel requirement; do not assume any downloaded quantization works with the existing local runtime. [Author model card](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy-MT2-7B). |

Hy-MT2's technical report describes about **1T tokens** of translation-oriented mid-training, mixing monolingual and parallel data across general, domain, business and instruction tasks. Family-specific distillation and reinforcement learning follow. WMT25, FLORES and other modern-language results use learned/automated metrics including XCOMET and GEMBA; they do not measure Pahlavi. The inspected report does not establish Pahlavi examples, a Pahlavi-specific corpus quantity, or expert Pahlavi evaluation. Its modern-language benchmark advantage is not evidence of an ancient-language advantage. [Hy-MT2 technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22064).

## What ancient-language successes actually demonstrate

| Effort | Actual task, representation and scale | Transfer limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Original MITRA / Gemma-2-MITRA** | Gemma-2 9B domain adaptation: 4.4B Buddhist-domain tokens, including multilingual parallel material; two pretraining epochs on eight A100s over about four weeks, followed by translation instruction tuning. Its mined instruction material and document-level design differ substantially from a few thousand isolated sentence pairs. The paper reports repetitive hallucinations with one sentence-level supervision approach and improved behavior using its alternative instructions. [MITRA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06400v1). | This is precedent for domain data and training design, not evidence that another 9B checkpoint can read Pahlavi. Do not attach the old paper's experiments to the newer 22B-token Qwen checkpoint. Reproducing that training scale is outside this project's budget. |
| **Akkadian CNN translation, 2023** | Akkadian→English from scholarly transliteration or Unicode cuneiform text, not directly from tablet photographs. ORACC data comprise 8,056 tablets, mostly Neo-Assyrian; transliteration task has 56,160 sentence/line pairs, with 50,544 training examples. Reported BLEU is 37.47 for transliteration and 36.52 for Unicode input; shorter passages are easier. Code/data and a demo are linked. Total parameters and a deployable-weight license were not verified in this bounded inspection. [PNAS Nexus paper](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/2/5/pgad096/7147349). | Language, target and corpus size differ. Random sentence splitting does not establish work-disjoint generalization. Corpus BLEU cannot certify a particular disputed reading, and these scores cannot be compared numerically with this project's acceptance rate. |
| **TabletCraft / CuneiScribe, 2026** | ByT5-base, 581M parameters; 58,126 parallel sentences become approximately 116K bidirectional examples. Model input/output use transliteration; Unicode conversion and tablet rendering are subsequent components. On 2,812 held-out Akkademia validation examples it reports 49.1/48.5 BLEU for Akkadian→English/reverse. Expert evaluation is future work; the prior CNN comparison uses a different split. The paper links code/weights, but the artifact and license were not independently verified here. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02609), [author repository](https://github.com/geoffreywang1117/cuneiscribe). | Useful evidence that a small byte-level model can work with substantial aligned data in one narrow domain. It neither supports Pahlavi nor removes the need for philological checking. Its generated reverse translations are not authentic historical evidence. |
| **Aeneas; Ithaca** | Aeneas uses Latin transcription plus optional inscription imagery for restoration, dating, attribution and finding parallels. Its dataset has 176,861 inscriptions and 16M characters. Ithaca similarly addresses ancient Greek restoration/attribution. These are not general ancient→modern translation systems. [Aeneas Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09292-5), [Ithaca Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04448-z). | Finding plausible missing text is different from determining a faithful Persian translation. The transferable idea is to show relevant parallels and competing hypotheses to a scholar, not to substitute a restoration checkpoint. Model size/license investigation is unnecessary for this excluded task class and was not performed. |
| **PahGen** | Directly relevant language, but English→Pahlavi with lexical/morphological processing and GPT-4o, rather than a demonstrated downloadable Pahlavi→Persian checkpoint. This was already in September 25 research; the lead's fresh full-paper review covers its filtered vocabulary and restricted evaluation. [Primary paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.loresmt-1.16/). | Architectural inspiration for lexicon/grammar-supported generation; no reverse-direction or unknown-reading proof. Not a newly discovered alternative model. |

An earlier Buddhist Chinese evaluation used five experts on 182 translations and found retrieval useful. This supports checking evidence-conditioned translation with humans; it does not validate the current Qwen release, Pahlavi, or automatic judges as gold. [NLP4DH primary paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.nlp4dh-1.12/).

## Consequences for this project

1. **Retain the current model as the comparison baseline.** The fresh evidence supports testing MITRA-QWEN's historical-text specialization and Hy-MT2's Persian/terminology specialization separately. Neither has demonstrated both requirements. Avoid a chain through English merely because a specialist outputs English: that adds another source of error and requires separate validation.
2. **Compare source fidelity before training.** After runtime/budget readiness, use the same untouched evaluation sources and the same permitted evidence packets across models, with each model's documented prompt. Score negation, grammatical roles, named entities, omissions, invented propositions and uncertainty using blinded expert review where available. Separate closed-book from evidence-assisted runs. Existing reused PALREF items remain a regression set, not fresh confirmatory evidence; development-assisted tuning needs an untouched final set.
3. **The hardware permits investigation, not a speed guarantee.** Nominal BF16 weights are roughly 18GB for 9B and 14–16GB for the marketed 7B model; nominal 4-bit weights are about one quarter of those sizes before runtime, quantization and cache overhead. An 8GB GPU plus approximately 64GB RAM and CPU offload therefore makes small alternatives plausible, but exact engine support, fidelity after quantization and 10–20-minute latency remain unmeasured. The existing 31B path also needs its own local runtime measurement; tolerable waiting time weakens the argument for sacrificing quality solely to shrink the model.
4. **Protect the remaining $13.25 authorized compute envelope** under the stated $25 cumulative cap ($11.75 already used). The actual account balance is unknown. Literature evidence does not justify spending this envelope on another domain-pretraining campaign or a speculative model switch. A100 80GB capacity does not resolve data quality, transfer validity or evaluation independence. This reassessment launched no jobs and incurred no model-inference spend.

Negative finding is bounded: no inspected primary source documents a released model beating the current model on Pahlavi→Persian. This is not an exhaustive proof that no such model exists. Public release/license claims above are metadata observations, not installed-runtime verification.
