Three iterations, effectively flat. The upward step from 1 to 2 falls within single-passage noise on a 15-item set.
1. Loss weighting error — Critical
contextual_train.py applies 20× the gradient pressure to contextual targets versus standard translation tokens. Every training run executed under this configuration has systematically under-trained the primary translation objective. This is not a tuning choice: it is a bug. All prior experiment conclusions are contaminated by it.
2. Corpus skew — High
One work accounts for ~49% of 2,237 qualified training pairs. At that concentration the model is not generalising across Pahlavi registers — it is learning the idiolect of a single text. Generalisation failure to other works is structurally determined by the data, not by insufficient training steps.
3. Exposure bias — High
Good NLL alongside poor free-generation quality is the textbook signature of teacher-forcing overfitting. The model has learned next-token prediction conditioned on gold prefixes; it has not learned to sustain coherent autoregressive output. This does not resolve by training longer under the same regime.
4. Evaluation rubric calibration — Medium
Classifying a single participant-role error as a “critical failure” is appropriate for deployment review; it is punishing as an iteration signal on a 15-passage dev set. One such error removes ~7% of available score. The rubric is causing metric saturation: there is no resolution at the performance range currently being measured.
5. Dev set size — Medium
15 passages produce confidence intervals wide enough that a 1-passage difference is indistinguishable from variance. No result on this set can be taken as evidence that one configuration outperforms another.
- Qualified training pairs: 2,237
- Dominant work share: ~49%
- Effective coverage: heavily biased toward one source register and syntactic style
- No evidence (in retrieved files) of deduplication audit, inter-work style normalisation, or vocabulary coverage analysis across the corpus
- Exposure bias confirmed behaviorally: NLL tracks well; autoregressive output does not
The current loop — adjust mixing ratios and hyperparameters, retrain, evaluate on the same 15 passages — has no remaining headroom. It cannot surface the loss-weighting error’s contribution, cannot compensate for corpus skew, and cannot distinguish real improvement from noise at this evaluation scale.
Continuing this loop before fixing the structural issues wastes training budget and produces unreliable evidence.
Viable redirections in priority order:
1. Fix the loss weighting bug before any new training run — otherwise the gradient error is still the dominant factor in all results.
2. Rebalance the corpus — cap the dominant work at ≤20% of training pairs per epoch; assess whether even low-quality augmentation from secondary sources improves cross-work generalisation.
3. Address exposure bias in the training regime — scheduled sampling or minimum-risk training in the final training phase rather than pure teacher-forcing throughout.
4. Decouple the evaluation rubric — score translation fluency/adequacy separately from protocol compliance; use the former as the primary iteration signal at this stage.
5. Expand the dev set before treating any result as meaningful — under 50 passages, claims about improvement cannot be separated from noise.
