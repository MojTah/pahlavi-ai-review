## What the three requested abilities require

1. **Known isolated words:** published passage translations supply contextual evidence, not automatically word-to-word labels. The qualified payload still contains only nine single-whitespace-token inputs. First establish a traceable word/sense inventory with attested spans, morphology, alternative readings and context limits. Reuse retained TRAIN witnesses; do not turn a Codex draft gloss or the model's own guess into supervision. The five previously identified TRAIN-only dictionary entries are a narrow starting resource, not validated coverage of the language. A future word-level test must count ambiguous/missing-reference entries explicitly rather than force a single answer.
2. **New compositions of known words:** test who did what to whom, negation, quantities, scope, ellipsis and sense selection in held-out passages. Vocabulary overlap is not proof of compositional novelty or known senses. Current genuine DEV is useful for diagnosis, but it is not a specialist-validated composition benchmark. If later supervision is justified, prefer authentic TRAIN constructions with faithful whole translations; synthetic role reversals or segmented gloss labels need independent linguistic validation.
3. **New inscriptions/unknown lexicon:** the achievable near-term role is an evidence-assisted research aid: preserve supported meaning and locate uncertainty without inventing a reading. Correctly translating familiar literary transcription cannot establish unknown-word discovery. Scholarly transcription already contains editorial decisions. Typed native script, transliteration, reading damaged signs and interpreting a reading are separate capabilities; no current result validates the native-script/image stages. A proposed new meaning ultimately needs independent textual/philological evidence.

The withdrawn staged24/30 panel must remain out of tuning because it contains original TEST records. Do not repair that limitation by silently reclassifying it or using PAL-REF failures as new “known-word” lessons.
