# Candidate semantic-assessment interpretation v1

This candidate is written after seeing the original disagreements. It applies to one post-hoc diagnostic reassessment of existing exposed outputs. It is not a prospectively frozen primary merit and cannot retroactively replace original scores. Source/reference/constraint bytes and old ratings remain unchanged. Numeric merit, categories, judgment vocabulary and continuation thresholds stay the same; the interpretation of contextual ambiguity is made explicit here.

1. Judge substantive propositions and relations, not reference wording or fluent style. Before acceptance, account for the required event, participants, relationships, negation/modality, essential names/quantities and restrictions. Lexical presence alone does not prove their relationships survive.
2. Permit ordinary Persian ellipsis and a defensible source-supported sense when the saved target wording and supplied context support that meaning. Do not demand an overt finite predicate, an exact reference phrase, every bracketed expansion, or an extra explanation of an allowed technical term. Conversely, do not reconstruct a missing substantive proposition solely because it is present in the expected answer. In the reason, distinguish what the target conveys from what the source/reference helps interpret.
3. Distinguish an explicit contradiction/unsupported substitution, a demonstrated substantive omission, and an unresolved attachment or sense. If faithful and materially different readings both remain defensible and the supplied evidence does not settle their recoverability, use `uncertain` for the affected category and whole judgment. Do not award acceptance, but do not assert a proven error or participant reversal from ambiguity alone. A separate demonstrated error can still make the whole output an error. This explicit ambiguity decision procedure is a candidate operational interpretation, not proven equivalent to every historical assessment.
4. Preserve all explicitly permitted published alternatives. Two alternatives already allowed by the case constraint are not an error or uncertainty merely because both exist. Optional explanatory notes are not required meaning. No reference omission or faulty reading is presumed without evidence; unresolved source questions stay unresolved.
5. Technical transliteration may preserve an established label; unusual spelling or literal liability phrasing needs a supported reason before being declared a different concept. Technical-label retention, its quantity/grade and the liable person are separate checks. Ordinary untranslated meaning words do not automatically become permitted technical terms. A materially unsupported replacement can fail even when its surface grammar is clearer.
6. Use the original critical-error definition. A proved reversal of prohibition/obligation, material participant roles, essential name/quantity or invented event-changing content is critical. Awkwardness, an unspecified attachment or an uncertain concept is not by itself a proved critical reversal. Uncertainty is never acceptance.

These clauses clarify evidence requirements already present in the merit; clause3 supplies a more explicit ambiguous-reading decision procedure. Making all grammatical links overt, demanding bracketed translator additions, or changing which published readings are allowed would change the assessment rule, and is not adopted here. Future primary adoption would require a separately reviewed version and prospective protocol.
