## Evidence that controls this decision

Our fixed resource is 2,237 qualified pairs from 76 works, with 37,492 source whitespace terms. These are provisionally reviewed parallel targets, not expert-certified gold. One work supplies 1,099 rows; that concentration is already known and does not require another census. Current input is scholarly transcription, not native-script recognition or a demonstrated unknown-word decipherment system.

The qualified Gemma checkpoint remains the reference. The latest matched control/candidate both received 48 additional updates; the contextual arm used 12 anchors from nine works. Both fresh reviewers accepted 0/15 whole DEV translations for either arm, and the candidate increased constrained critical errors. That closes this recipe, not contextual supervision in general. The older 16–17/40 PAL ratings involve different cases and panels and cannot be subtracted from 0/15.

Teacher-forced target likelihood improved on familiar passages, but supplied answer prefixes are not free translation. Earlier TRAIN recall already exposed whole-meaning failures despite preserved local glosses/functions. We have not measured the latest adapters' auxiliary-task generation, have not established an optimization defect, and have not identified the model's capability ceiling. |---|---|---|

| **Linguistic structure can help when its representation and labels are reliable.** Ancient Greek interlinear work found strong morphology-embedding results, while simply appending tags was weaker. | Keep source-aligned analysis as a conditional future approach, distinct from our phrase-target pilot. | Gold morphology, word-aligned translations and random verses from one corpus differ greatly from our problem. | **Linguistically plausible augmentation often loses to controls or simple noise.** | Do not generate large quantities of synthetic Pahlavi grammar drills from intuition. | Effects depend on language, dataset size and transformation; the study is not evidence that all augmentation fails. ## Ranked next actions

1. **Prepare one NLLB-1.3B adaptation comparison.** The open question is whether a multilingual translation specialist trained on the same evidence delivers a better attainable system than qualified Gemma. It is not a causal architecture experiment: foundation data, parameter count, adaptation and input formatting differ.
2. **Improve semantic evidence in parallel where it adds information.** Prioritize independently attested predicate senses, participant roles, scope and technical meanings across useful works. Preserve citation uncertainty. Specialist calibration of the existing rubric remains necessary for science-grade accuracy claims; it does not require replacing the fixed merit.
3. **Skip the paid auxiliary-mastery reload for now.** Candidate/control × 12 existing tasks would be a new 24-response diagnostic, not a duplicate of whole-translation recall. However, either result still leaves the failed recipe closed and does not choose between Gemma and NLLB. Reopen it only if a specified outcome would change an actual pending intervention. Its unresolved status remains in the record.
4. **Keep structured analysis and source-contrastive decoding conditional.** A source→analysis→translation system needs trustworthy analysis and a test separating correct supplied analyses from automatically generated ones. It is not equivalent to asking for fluent reasoning. Contrastive decoding is a distinct inference experiment, not another ordinary prompt comparison.
5. **Defer CPT, synthetic self-training, DPO/RL and hard dictionary constraints.** No sufficiently large additional clean source corpus or validated Pahlavi teacher/reward has been established. Improving term inclusion, perplexity or judge reward alone would not establish better translation. Do not launch a combination of methods simply because each has a positive paper.

## Additional evidence and cautions

**Active selection:** HUDS studies BART domain adaptation from 4.5M WMT pairs, selecting 1,000 in-domain pairs per round for ten rounds. Three-seed medicine BLEU was 34.58 versus 32.70 random; its reported neural-metric gain over random was much smaller. This is simulated labeling from existing parallel data, not demonstrated Pahlavi expert-cost savings. Borrow the principle of useful diversity plus uncertainty, not a new clustering infrastructure or its fixed mixture. [Methods and Tables 1–2/6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.206.pdf).

**Retrieval inside decoding:** DLUT's WMT24 system reports +2.33 average validation BLEU from token-level kNN after other adaptation stages. It used 22K–50K bitext pairs per direction, substantial monolingual data, 8×3090 training and datastores of roughly 0.5–1.4M entries. This is different from our failed prompt assistance, but adds a datastore and decoder integration without demonstrating transfer at our scale. Defer it until a stable translation-specialist baseline exists. [System paper, Tables 1–3](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wmt-1.63.pdf).

**Cross-study comparisons:** FRED's ACL2026 analysis found strong association between a nearest-neighbor retrieval proxy and reported low-resource MT scores. Its heterogeneous corpora, nonstandard fertility definition and proxy limitations prevent causal conclusions. Use it as a warning that impressive ancient-language BLEU may depend on repetition, representation and prior exposure. Do not replace our merit or import its score as a new benchmark. [Methods and limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.29.pdf).

The 2026 TACL limited-data survey was inspected as a discovery/organization aid, not used as direct experimental proof for a universal size, rank or data threshold. Practical competition accounts and LoRA guidance already examined on 25 September remain explicitly lower-strength, author-reported evidence; they do not override controlled project results. This review did not re-download their models or datasets.
