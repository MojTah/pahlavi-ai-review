# Learning objectives after the contextual pilot

Research date: 2026-09-28. Status: **PASS — bounded primary-source review**, not approval for a model run. Five full-text studies were inspected. No model, dataset or reference-body download; no new labels, inference, training, credentials or held-out answer inspection. Classic Codex: a bounded literature-to-decision review, with no implementation.

The evidence does **not** identify exposure bias, missing prerequisites, or insufficient updates as this project's established failure cause. It supports separating three questions: whether an adapter raises the probability of a supplied answer, whether it can generate the trained task's answer without that prefix, and whether that learning transfers to whole translation. Another objective is not yet a justified cure.

## What the project has and has not measured

The qualified step-280 Gemma adapter used 2,237 pairs, approximately 37,492 source whitespace terms, with approximately 49% of rows from one work. Its 20-parent conditional-fit probe reduced correct-target mean NLL from 4.299214 to 0.884414. A fixed unpaired source increased NLL in every parent, but the adapter's mean mismatch penalty was smaller than the base's: 3.697206 versus 4.486227. Gold prefixes expose target information, and the unpaired sources also differ in length/content; this is evidence of conditional fit, not improved faithfulness or free-generation quality. [Fit report](<[USER_HOME]/Documents/ChatGPT/Pahlavi language/experiments/train-fit-20260927/REPORT.md>).

The separate seen-TRAIN recall run completed 12 paired parents: both reviewers accepted 5 with the training prompt and 4 with the evaluation prompt. One subsequent generation failed at its cap, leaving 15 outputs unattempted; these are not representative accuracy estimates over all 20 selected parents. [Recall report](<[USER_HOME]/Documents/ChatGPT/Pahlavi language/experiments/train-recall-20260927/REPORT.md>).

The new contextual treatment used 12 anchors from 9 works, each exposed four times within 48 updates, against a matched whole-translation control. Both arms achieved 0/15 whole-translation acceptances on the latest DEV review; candidate critical errors were worse on constrained cases for both reviewers. The intervention changed task, target length and the distribution of token weights within equal-parent loss. **Its exact auxiliary-task generation was never measured after training.** Earlier recall/fit used the original adapter and translation tasks, so neither answers that narrow question. This gap does not invalidate the failed DEV result or reopen that recipe. [Contextual report](<[USER_HOME]/Documents/ChatGPT/Pahlavi language/experiments/contextual-supervision-20260927/REPORT.md>).

## Five informative primary studies

### 1. Xue et al., ACL 2026: incomplete learning after SFT

The paper evaluates Qwen 7B/14B and LLaMA2 7B/13B, with benchmark datasets ranging from 7,787 ARC items to 187,995 MedMCQA items. Its diagnostic converts responses into multiple-choice questions; it reports 15.3% ± 2.1% unlearned items across ten datasets after convergence, then selects 1,000 severe cases. Repeated answers and confidence support its operational attribution, not direct access to latent knowledge. Table 4 reports MedQA learning rate 65.3% after two SFT epochs, 66.8% after ten, and 82.1% after knowledge-enhanced CPT plus SFT. Crucially, Appendix F reports OLMo2-7B declines on every listed broad benchmark after CPT, including MMLU −3.5 and GPQA −6.8 in its reported percentage-change units. The inspected text does not provide a reproducible GPU-hour budget.

**Transfer limit:** multiple-choice recovery is easier/different than generating qualified Pahlavi translations. External retrieval and GPT-based judgments underpin parts of the diagnosis. This supports measuring the trained task before explaining failure; it does not establish our missing knowledge or justify CPT/extra epochs. [Full paper, §§3–4, Tables 4–5 and 14](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1393.pdf).

### 2. Wang and Sennrich, ACL 2020: minimum-risk training under domain shift

Six-layer encoder/decoder Transformers were first trained by maximum likelihood, then fine-tuned with minimum-risk training (MRT), sampling four candidate translations per input and using sentence BLEU loss. Data included 180,000 German–English IWSLT pairs and 100,000 German–Romansh law-domain pairs. Domain-shift results average three training runs; approximately 3,600 manual annotations cover 12 configurations. IWSLT BLEU rose 34.7→35.2; average out-of-domain gains with label smoothing were +0.8 German–English and +0.7 German–Romansh. In-domain quality did not consistently improve. In one German–English comparison, hallucinations at beam four fell 33%→26%; larger beams remained problematic. Appendix A gives architecture and optimization settings, not an end-to-end GPU-hour cost.

**Transfer limit:** sequence objectives can help source detachment, but these datasets are vastly larger and their BLEU reward is not our critical-error rubric. Four candidate generations introduce real extra computation. We lack a validated Pahlavi semantic reward; optimizing surface overlap could reward a fluent wrong translation. [Full paper, §§3–4 and Appendix A](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.326.pdf).

### 3. He et al.: exposure bias versus self-recovery

This study intervenes on generation prefixes rather than assuming errors must accumulate. A 16-layer Transformer-XL and a three-layer LSTM are studied on WikiText-103; the real-data model uses approximately 900,000 sentences/50 million words, reserving the other half for reference distributions. A separate synthetic experiment trains a four-layer model on samples from a larger model. Measurements use 10,000 samples per run and ten random seeds. Replacing model prefixes with data prefixes yields approximately 1–2% relative gains on the authors' distributional and text metrics, without steadily increasing damage for prefixes up to 100 tokens. Strongly corrupted prefixes hurt more, but models partially recover. Wall-clock training cost is not supplied in the inspected setup.

**Transfer limit:** open-ended English continuation is weakly constrained; Pahlavi translation must preserve a particular source. This negative finding neither refutes the MT results above nor diagnoses our model. It rules out treating the general teacher-forcing/free-generation discrepancy as sufficient proof of an exposure-bias failure. [Full text, §§5–6, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.10617).

### 4. Sennrich, Vamvas and Mohammadshahi, EACL 2024: contrast the source at decoding

Without retraining, the method penalizes tokens also probable under a randomly paired source; a second variant penalizes unwanted output languages. On 57 mostly low-resource directions, M2M-100 418M and SMaLL-100 gain +1.3/+1.1 chrF2 from source contrast, with further +0.4/+0.2 from language contrast. The combined source-and-language variants reduce the heuristic count of outputs with chrF2 below 10 by 67–83%; this proxy conflates hallucination and wrong-language output. The paper's Llama demonstration concerns language contrast, not these 57-direction source-contrast results. High-resource averages deteriorate: for M2M-100, source contrast changes chrF2 61.3→60.8 and spBLEU 37.2→36.4. Parameters were tuned on one excluded direction. Contrast requires additional conditional model evaluations; no comparable end-to-end cost is established here.

**Transfer limit:** this is an inference intervention, not evidence that training on arbitrary negative sources is effective. It motivates separating source-conditioned probability from generic Persian plausibility, while illustrating both metric sensitivity and regressions. It does not authorize changing our frozen decoding policy or treating random mismatches as qualified semantic negatives. [Full paper, §§2–3, Table 1](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-short.4.pdf).

### 5. Rafailov et al., 2023: what DPO actually supplies

DPO fits preferred/rejected response pairs relative to a reference policy; it avoids an explicit reward model and online RL rollouts during optimization. The experiments include GPT-2-large sentiment control, GPT-J 6B summarization and Pythia 2.8B dialogue. Sentiment supervision uses four completions for 25,000 prefixes, yielding six classifier-labeled pairs per prefix; the dialogue dataset contains 170,000 transcripts. On summarization, GPT-4-rated wins against reference summaries reach approximately 61% for DPO versus 57% for PPO. Human comparisons prefer DPO over PPO 58% of the time, but the paper also shows evaluator-prompt sensitivity. A simpler unlikelihood baseline fails to produce meaningful responses on complex tasks. Default DPO batch size is 64; the paper does not establish a tiny-budget Pahlavi compute requirement.

**Transfer limit:** avoiding RL does not remove the need for reliable preferences. Twelve qualified phrase targets are not a preference corpus; neither current model outputs nor an unvalidated automatic judge supplies trustworthy rejected translations. DPO's preference wins are not source-faithfulness guarantees. [Full text, §§6–7 and Appendices B–D](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18290v3).

## Ranked next decision — project inference

1. **Immediate, non-paid: decide which uncertainty would change the next action.** The unmeasured quantity is recovery of the exact 12 auxiliary tasks after the contextual run, not another audit of the already qualified scopes. Freeze this decision rule using existing artifacts: if learning the auxiliary task would still leave the Gemma recipe closed, do not buy a reload solely to explain its failure. Preserve the question as an unresolved limitation. No new annotation or broad corpus audit is needed to reach this decision.

2. **Only if continued Gemma mechanism work remains a live option: one zero-update mastery check.** The smallest informative comparison is the 12 original auxiliary prompts under candidate and matched-control adapters: 24 fresh, source-only, first-attempt generations, with the existing qualified targets used locally for scoped review. No answer prefixes or multiple-choice distractors. Report all parents and works, qualified uncertainty, omissions and failures. This measures seen-task recovery, not translation merit. If candidate improves auxiliary recovery but not DEV translation, learning occurred without demonstrated transfer. If it fails auxiliary recovery, the intervention never established the intended competence; that still does not prove too few epochs or prescribe a larger dose. Equal success would weaken the claim that candidate-specific auxiliary learning was necessary. Existing fit/recall cannot substitute for this test, but those older tests already make a broad “can it learn anything?” rerun redundant.

3. **A fair NLLB-600M challenger need not wait for that diagnostic.** The reviewed evidence contains no comparison establishing that another Gemma reload has more expected practical value than this structurally different challenger. The mastery check isolates a mechanism; the challenger tests a different pretrained architecture and adaptation route. If the objective is finding a usable translator and Gemma continuation is closed, prioritize the challenger's non-paid compatibility/protocol/cost readiness over additional Gemma explanation. Its training is a separate budget decision, not an evidence-backed superiority claim. Use the same qualified TRAIN and unchanged whole-translation merit; do not grant it easier targets or credit auxiliary scores as successes.

4. **Conditional objective contrast, lower priority:** only after independently qualified TRAIN-only preference pairs or a semantic sequence reward exist, compare positive-target SFT with the same positive supervision plus a source-faithfulness preference/contrastive objective. Bind source, scope and uncertainty; reject whole-sentence negatives that might be valid alternatives. Keep initialization, positive data, parent sampling and stopping fixed; account separately for negative tokens and generation cost rather than claiming equal updates mean equal compute. DPO is computationally simpler than online RL, but the preferred-only SFT control is essential. MRT is a later alternative only if its reward distinguishes source-critical mistakes; BLEU/chrF alone is insufficient here. No present evidence admits either experiment now, and no automatic epoch, model or objective sweep follows failure.

Any later translation comparison retains the existing 15 whole/9 constrained cases, separate fresh blinded reviewers, fixed first-attempt denominators and critical-error safeguards. Auxiliary mastery remains a separate TRAIN diagnostic. No DEV/PALREF answers, rejected outputs or reviewer explanations become training preferences or reward calibration data. Repeated DEV use remains development evidence, not fresh confirmatory generalization.
