# Ancient-language translation: verified research checkpoint

Date: 2026-09-25. Scope: Pahlavi/Middle Persian text translation with English and Farsi, including unseen passages. OCR is excluded. Compute, data acquisition, and expert availability are requirements to quantify, not reasons to reject an approach.

## Main conclusion

MITRA is the strongest verified full-scale ancient-language translation precedent in this research lane. Its transferable approach is language/domain adaptation plus parallel evidence and translation-specific training. It is not evidence that its released weights already understand Pahlavi. GrammaMT supplies a complementary, directly testable way to expose lexical and grammatical evidence. Neither restoration accuracy nor fluent target-language generation establishes faithful translation of unseen Pahlavi.

## Evidence actually inspected

Read the local September 22 strategy review; the MITRA January paper methods/evaluation; current MITRA root/v2 repository READMEs; the released Gemma model card; GrammaMT's ACL paper, including prompting variants and GlossLM requirements; LingoLLM's repository README; PahGen's paper; and primary Apollo/Aeneas descriptions. No model was downloaded, trained, or run. A public GitHub API attempt failed with a connection authentication error; its escalation was aborted. Therefore implementation files, commit SHAs, and end-to-end reproducibility were **not** verified here. Do not label this report a completed code audit.

## 1. MITRA: actual translation and the strongest adaptation precedent

The January 2026 study combines Gemma 2 9B with 4.4 billion domain tokens containing monolingual and parallel material. Continued pretraining lasted two epochs, approximately four weeks on eight A100s. It then used 10,000 multidirectional and 30,979 document-level instructions mined with Claude 3.5 Sonnet, followed by four fine-tuning epochs. The authors report less repetitive hallucination with these instruction examples than with gold sentence-pair instruction tuning; this supports testing synthetic instructions conditionally, not treating them as inherently beneficial or harmful. Evaluation covers Sanskrit, Pali, Tibetan and Buddhist Chinese into English; the main comparisons use Gemini-based GEMBA, with additional metrics for Chinese. Reverse translation was not independently assessed in this paper. Its first mining release's 100-pair manual sample was 73% fully aligned, 16% partial and 11% wrong. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06400v1)

**Available artifacts.** The verified 9B [translation model card](https://huggingface.co/buddhist-nlp/gemma-2-mitra-it) gives an explicit translation prompt, a special newline marker and stop token. This is an actual translation checkpoint. Its general applicability and exact licensing conditions still require review before reuse. The current [repository](https://github.com/dharmamitra/mitra-parallel) advertises a newer Qwen3.5 family; the linked collection could not be opened here, so its model contents and quality are unresolved.

**Current corpus documentation.** The [v2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dharmamitra/mitra-parallel/main/v2/README.md) records 1,693,730 aligned records / 2,338,400 segment pairs, with provenance and alignment scores. It describes embedding anchors, monotone-chain detection, VecAlign reconstruction, dictionary/density/length filters and an aligner-drift screen. It also records evaluation decontamination and exact deduplication. Those pipeline claims were read from documentation; the corresponding implementation was not inspected. Pali is excluded from that v2 release.

**Transfer to Pahlavi.** Build a comparable evidence base from permissibly usable, corrected monolingual texts, scholarly translations, dictionaries, grammar and philological commentary. Keep transcription, manuscript spelling, lemma and contextual sense distinct. Adapt a strong base and then train translation instructions at sentence and passage levels. A borrowed Buddhist-language checkpoint is a comparator, not an automatic Pahlavi initialization winner. Determine that empirically against broader multilingual bases. More compute enables this experiment; trustworthy Pahlavi coverage and independent evaluation remain necessary.

## 2. GrammaMT and GlossLM: make the linguistic evidence explicit

[GrammaMT, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1447.pdf) compares glossed demonstrations, model self-glossing, and externally generated glosses. Its major unseen-language gains depend on the third approach: a separate language-specific GlossLM supplies lexical/morphological annotations. Self-glossing gains are smaller and inconsistent. The paper explicitly uses variants that do not receive the desired translation when generating glosses. It identifies [GlossLM code](https://github.com/foltaProject/glosslm) and [released models](https://huggingface.co/lecslab), including language-specific fine-tunes; this does not establish a Pahlavi model. Translation prompting can be training-free while the glossing component still depends on substantial prior supervision.

**Reusable component:** the source/gloss/translation representation and controlled prompting comparison. A Pahlavi gloss should encode independently established meanings and grammar, including unresolved alternatives, rather than paraphrasing a hidden reference translation. With approximately 463 English and 2,609 Farsi parallel pairs in the September 22 project review, existing pairs alone do not automatically provide morpheme-level supervision. Annotated examples, analysis conventions and expert adjudication must be obtained or built. GlossLM's exact architecture/configuration and a public GrammaMT implementation were not verified in this checkpoint.

## 3. LingoLLM: useful interface, incomplete portability evidence

The inspected [repository README](https://github.com/LeiLiLab/LingoLLM) exposes direct, few-shot and dictionary translation modes through `gen.py`; input is one sentence per line. Its Manchu demonstration uses a cached dictionary and GPT-4o. Creating a missing dictionary invokes Selenium against a language-specific dictionary site. The README still lists other-language data/evaluation scripts and fuller pipeline documentation as work to release. Consequently the repository is not a ready general-language adapter. Reuse the separation of translation, dictionary lookup and demonstrations, while building Pahlavi evidence and testing each component independently. `gen.py`, prompts, dictionary code and revisions were not read, so no implementation correctness or completeness claim is made. [Primary LingoLLM publication](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.925/)

## 4. Direct Pahlavi evidence and restoration boundaries

[PahGen](https://aclanthology.org/2025.loresmt-1.16.pdf) is direct English-to-Pahlavi research combining grammar-guided term processing with an LLM. The existing strategy review already establishes the limited, vocabulary-filtered evaluation and release-count discrepancy. It is valuable task-specific evidence, but no independently verified downloadable Pahlavi pretrained translation checkpoint emerged in this search. That is a bounded search outcome, not proof that none exists. The [Parsig-English release](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PahGen/Parsig-English) is a dataset, not model weights. Modern Persian ParsBERT is an encoder for language understanding, not a Middle Persian translator.

[Apollo](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22455v1) restores missing Greek spans; its approximately 80% documentary-papyrus headline is a top-20 short-gap restoration result. [Aeneas/Ithaca](https://deepmind.google/blog/aeneas-transforms-how-historians-connect-the-past/) restore and contextualize inscriptions. These projects offer evaluation and evidence-presentation ideas, but no demonstrated general Pahlavi translation capability.

## Prioritized next experiment

Prepare a work-separated, philologist-adjudicated challenge set covering both input languages, both output languages, unfamiliar natural passages, terminology, negation, argument roles and ambiguous readings. First compare strong translation candidates with: (1) no added evidence; (2) independently verified lexical senses; (3) the same senses plus grammatical glosses. Score critical meaning errors and answer coverage, not just overlap or fluency. Then compare the winning evidence format before and after domain adaptation and translation training, including a separately measured synthetic-instruction condition. Keep all test works and related copies out of training and retrieval. Report each direction and genre separately.

Open dependencies for a deeper wave: inspect and pin actual MITRA/GlossLM/LingoLLM implementations; verify the latest MITRA family; inventory usable Pahlavi monolingual and aligned scholarly material; define expert annotation/review requirements; and compare language adaptation versus translation supervision without conflating their objectives.
