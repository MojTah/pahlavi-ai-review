# Deeper transfer and data-augmentation evidence, 2026-09-25

Read-only research; no training or model downloads performed. Resource requirements below describe the evidence, not limits on the proposed project. None of these studies demonstrates reliable Pahlavi translation. The strongest transferable program combines specialist MT adaptation, retrieval of trusted examples and lexical evidence, and expert-checked structured augmentation, compared independently before combination.

## 1. Fine-tuned specialist MT can learn a genuinely unsupported language

Aycock et al., ICLR 2025, disentangle grammar explanations from translated examples for Kalamang, an unseen language. NLLB-1.3B trained on 1,239 grammar-book examples scores **34.2 chrF++ English→Kalamang / 28.6 reverse**; Gemini-1.5-Flash given those examples scores 26.6/33.1. Adding 400 authentic pairs improves NLLB to **38.7/36.9**. One backtranslation iteration instead yields 32.0/31.6: beneficial into English, harmful into Kalamang. Grammar explanations give little translation benefit beyond their parallel examples; this does not establish that structured grammatical supervision is useless. Their combined test contains only 100 sentences and lacks proficient-speaker human evaluation. [Paper, tables 2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19151v2).

**Code audit:** [XLR-MTOB](https://github.com/Sethjsa/XLR-MTOB) is MIT. I directly read `fine-tune/ft.py`, `finetune_nllb.py`, and both `nllb-1.3-dev500-*` configs. These are research artifacts requiring repair: `finetune_nllb.py` exits before training; `ft.py` contains commented language-extension code; configs pass `forced_bos_token="kgv"`, including the English-target configuration. The English→Kalamang config uses batch32, 16epochs, LR1e-4, length128, beam10. Do not claim these files reproduce the paper unchanged. [Training entrypoint](https://github.com/Sethjsa/XLR-MTOB/blob/main/fine-tune/ft.py), [configuration](https://github.com/Sethjsa/XLR-MTOB/blob/main/fine-tune/configs/nllb-1.3-dev500-ek.json).

**Pahlavi experiment, inference:** full-fine-tune NLLB, including larger variants, with a verified new language tag and supervised pairs; compare to the existing ByT5 and Qwen runs. Audit unknown characters and truncation for every accepted input representation. The evidence supports trying a translation-trained model even when Pahlavi is unsupported. NLLB weights have **CC-BY-NC-4.0** licensing, independently of MIT research code. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/nllb-200-distilled-1.3B).

## 2. Retrieval plus an expanded dictionary is an independently proven alternative

DiPMT++ (ACL Findings 2024) uses **4,944 Zhuang–Chinese pairs, a 16,031-word Zhuang dictionary, and three BM25-retrieved examples**, plus fuzzy matching, bilingual lexicon induction, and synonym expansion. The offline Qwen-72B-chat baseline improves from original dictionary prompting's **5.1/16.3 BLEU** to **16.4/27.3** Chinese→Zhuang/reverse. BM25 retrieval beats random examples and noisy POS-based retrieval. Zhuang is treated as unseen, with near-zero direct prompting performance; this is behavioral evidence, not an audit of every pretraining document. Its 200-item test has easy/medium/hard subsets. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.519.pdf).

[ZhuangBench](https://github.com/luciusssss/ZhuangBench) provides MIT code and data, including the augmented dictionaries and scripts. Its use of a large Chinese-capable backbone is material: substituting an arbitrary small model is not replication.

**Pahlavi experiment, inference:** retrieve trusted Pahlavi examples and alternative dictionary senses for a strong multilingual model; compare retrieval-only, dictionary-only, and combined prompts. Require preserved negation, argument roles, names, number, and modality. Use only training resources in retrieval during evaluation. English/Farsi capability and ambiguity handling must be established separately. This uses new combinations at inference without inventing synthetic Pahlavi labels.

## 3. Structured grammar can help through controlled synthetic training

**GrammarMT, September 2026 revision**, is distinct from GrammaMT. It replaces words in trusted sentence templates while consulting structured rules or grammar sections. Per configuration it uses 400 seeds and 5–20 variants, yielding roughly 1,900–7,660 synthetic pairs; Gemini-2.5-Flash is fine-tuned for three epochs. Kalamang improves over the seed-only baseline in 75% of configurations, Tuatschin in 59%, Mandan in 16%. Best gains are +8.8/+5.3/+3.3 chrF++; nevertheless Kalamang section-based inference scores **27.76**, exceeding the best synthetic model's 21.48. Mandan's mean falls from 13.09 to 10.44. Its NLLB baseline uses proxy zero-shot inference, not fine-tuning. An unresolved count inconsistency remains: Table 1 lists 761/981/921 extracted pairs, whereas §4.1 describes 500 test plus approximately 800 training pairs per language. [Paper v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22376v2).

**Code audit:** [GrammarMT](https://github.com/varunghat/GrammarMT) is MIT, with extraction, codification, generation, and Vertex SFT scripts. I read [sentence_generation.py](https://github.com/varunghat/GrammarMT/blob/main/scripts/sentence_generation.py) and [gemini_finetune.py](https://github.com/varunghat/GrammarMT/blob/main/scripts/gemini_finetune.py). The public generator samples dictionary replacements with `random.sample`/`random.choice`, while the paper describes semantic candidate ranking. The training script contains fixed language/data paths. This warrants reconciliation, not automatic distrust or blind execution.

**Pahlavi experiment, inference:** have an expert validate morphological rules and original templates; generate controlled changes to subjects, objects, tense, negation, and vocabulary, then validate both sides before training. Hold out entire templates and lexical combinations. Requirements: grammar/lexicon rights, substantial expert review, a strong generator, and independently adjudicated evaluation. This remains attractive when resources are unrestricted; greater quantity alone is not the demonstrated mechanism.

## 4. Full fine-tuning versus stronger adapters deserves a real comparison

AmericasNLP 2026 tests NLLB600M across 13 languages, including Chatino 357 pairs, Ashaninka 3,883, Otomi 4,889, Bribri 7,508, and Wixarika 8,966. Ten languages are absent from NLLB's language inventory; new tags copy Spanish embeddings. Full fine-tuning averages **25.12 test chrF++**, OFT 25.06, LoRA 22.70. Full versus OFT is not statistically distinguishable. OFT targets all four attention projections, LoRA only q/v: this is not a mechanism-only comparison. Its initial ByT5 comparison also uses LoRA, so it does not settle full-tuned ByT5 quality. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.americasnlp-6.4.pdf).

The [public repository](https://github.com/drewstackhouse/peft-nmt-americas) contains training/config/results infrastructure; I inspected [model.py](https://github.com/drewstackhouse/peft-nmt-americas/blob/main/src/model.py): it adds tags, copies embeddings, checks tied weights and PEFT module matches. No explicit repository license was visible in the root. Use as evidence/reference until reuse rights are clear. The practical comparison is full FT versus LoRA versus OFT with matched training evidence and semantic evaluation, not an assumption that adapter efficiency implies better accuracy.

## Secondary leads and the generalization test

Mixed-language CPT (ACL 2021) denoises dictionary-substituted target-language monolingual text before parallel fine-tuning. It studies 10k parallel pairs plus 100k monolingual paragraphs, with roughly 30% token replacement and embeddings trainable. Gains exist, but many absolute BLEU scores remain low; all experimental languages already have tokenizer coverage. Crucially the [paper's promised repository is currently empty](https://github.com/zliucr/cpt-nmt). Treat this as a method to reimplement and test if useful, not ready reusable code. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.239.pdf).

For the user's exact unseen-combination requirement, [DBCA](https://github.com/aalto-speech/dbca) constructs translation splits with known lexical/grammatical atoms but unfamiliar combinations. Its existing experiments depend on morphological/dependency annotations and are not Pahlavi-ready. **Inference:** adapt the evaluation principle using expert annotations, alongside held-out works/genres and an independently written contrastive test. Score semantic errors and calibrated uncertainty, not only chrF/BLEU or agreement with one English wording. Reversing pairs does not create new independent source passages.
