## Fifteen translation and grammar insights

1. **Enclitic agents must be resolved before supplying a subject.** In `parsig:152004026`, `u-šān` can govern both `ōzad` and `darrīd`: “they killed and tore apart.” Making the second verb passive loses the shared agent. In `parsig:152003001`, the note specifically distinguishes agent `-š` from additive `-iz`.
2. **Past participial constructions do not map mechanically onto modern Persian active pasts.** `kard ēstēd`, `dād ēstēd`, and related forms can mark resultant states. In `parsig:152003065`, the note advocates a passive “were created” because no agent is expressed. An inferred creator should stay visibly supplied.
3. **Modal infinitives and verbal nouns encode obligation or permission.** `abāyēd ...`, `šāyēd ...`, `pādixšā ...` and `-išn` constructions require different translations. `parsig:138009011` is an invitation obligation in English, while Persian renders an event; `parsig:151010004` says wisdom without goodness must not be counted as wisdom.
4. **Negative and exception scope is decisive.** `ma` is a prohibition; `nē` commonly negates a proposition. `bē ka` in `parsig:138003015` is read as “except when” in English but effectively as a necessary certainty condition in Persian, reversing the rule's default. Preserve the two readings until adjudicated.
5. **Manichaean `hēb` requires an optative/jussive English register.** `parsig:515000001`–`parsig:515000004` repeatedly use `hēb` and `ma hēb`. “Let them…”/“They should…” matches the injunction better than an unmarked prediction. This is a translation observation, not endorsement of the prescriptions.
6. **`ī` is not always modern ezafe.** It links adjectives, relatives, substantivized clauses and genealogy; in inscriptions it can connect patronymics. `parsig:213001001` requires disentangling dedicator, beneficiary and genealogy, while Zand notes explicitly revise izafa placement (`parsig:301004001`, `parsig:301004003`, `parsig:301004005`).
7. **`kū` and `čiyōn` have several discourse roles.** Direct speech, complement, consequence, gloss and comparison must stay distinct. Braced `kū` glosses in `parsig:301004008` explain Mithra's many eyes and ears; they are not part of one uninterrupted base invocation. `čiyōn` frames genuine comparisons in `parsig:133000106`–`107` and `parsig:152034022`.
8. **Present/future and narrative tense need genre context.** `parsig:130000018` has a past-form transcription and Persian “killed,” but English future “will kill”; `parsig:130000027` similarly differs. The calendrical sequence blends past myth and eschatology. A polished translation must state the edition choice rather than silently regularize it.
9. **Comparatives can carry a comparison omitted by translation.** `parsig:117000123` is rendered in French as restraint being better than all the page's feats, whereas Persian describes a good trait in accomplishing them. `-tar`/`-tom` also organize the compact contrasts of `parsig:133000032`–`038` and `parsig:150000009`–`012`.
10. **Nominal clauses need conservative copula/subject supply.** The compressed question-answer register of `parsig:133000023`–`026` is clear without expanding it into prose assertions. At `parsig:137001006`, the competing `har dō hēnd` / `har dōwān mēnōg` readings materially affect “both are” versus “both spirits have” finitude/infinity.
11. **Numbers are textual evidence, not a cleanup opportunity.** `parsig:136004021` reads twenty, thirty, and twenty-one in transcription, Persian, and French respectively. `parsig:152003038`–`039` differ by factors of ten. Retain the stated number in each layer and flag it; do not average or choose the plausible number.
12. **Polysemy can cause major false friends.** `puhl` can be punishment, not automatically “bridge” (`parsig:152001016`, `parsig:152001022`); `ōš` means death in `parsig:134006012` but intelligence in `parsig:151047004`; `xwarrah` and `gyān` need context. `rōy` and `almās` in historic material lists need edition-specific identification (`parsig:152003069`, `parsig:503000004`).
13. **Headless relatives and list attachment change who/what is counted.** `parsig:150000027` yields wise physician / good wife / good relative in Persian versus wise man / good physician / virtuous wife in English. `parsig:132000051` groups four paired qualities; isolated word-counting obscures the rhetorical structure.
14. **Technical astronomical and calendrical words must retain their historical meanings.** At `parsig:152002022`, Ohrmazd is Jupiter in the planetary account, not simply the creator acting in a constellation. `bālist`/`nišēb` are rendered apogee/perigee in French but the zodiacal context raises exaltation/depression terminology; do not modernize this as orbital physics without a specialist check. `nēm-rōz` can mean noon or south (`parsig:152000001`).
15. **Variant apparatus and religious identity belong in the translation record.** `parsig:134004007` explicitly contrasts whole lexical readings, and `parsig:151056014`–`015` distinguish Pahlavi from Pazand-based editorial additions. `parsig:138009012` includes Avestan quotations; French layers in 117/136/152 remain French; 206 is Christian Middle Persian and 503/507/510/515/534 are Manichaean Middle Persian. Shared words do not prove identical doctrine or a switch to another Iranian language.

## Three original working renderings

These are short new renderings from the supplied romanized text, checked against the displayed editions. They are provisional translations, not independent readings of Pahlavi script.

**parsig:133000106–107 (abridged to the complete comparison).**

فارسی: «بخت و کوشش با هم چون تن و جان‌اند: تن بی‌جان پیکری ناتوان است و جان بی‌تن بادی دست‌نیافتنی. چون به هم بپیوندند، نیرومند و بسیار سودمند می‌شوند.»

English: “Fortune and action belong together like body and soul. Without soul, the body is powerless; without body, the soul is an intangible wind. Joined, they have strength and great usefulness.”

Note: `kunišn` is literally action; “کوشش” makes this reading idiomatic but is narrower, so «کنش» is preferable in a closer translation.

**parsig:150000019.**

فارسی: «از دو چیز شرم نکن: بیمار بودن، و خویشاوندی که تهیدست است.»

English: “Do not feel ashamed of being ill or of having a poor relative.”

Note: the impersonal `nē abāyēd` is converted to a direct singular injunction for natural prose; an academic version should use «نباید شرم داشت»/“One should not be ashamed.”

**parsig:152034022.**

فارسی: «خانه با سه چیز کامل می‌شود: زمین، دیوار و بام. در این همانندی، آفرینش زمین است، گسترش دین دیوار، و به انجام رساندن فرشگرد بام.»

English: “A house is completed by its ground, walls and roof. In this comparison, creation supplies the ground, the spread of the religion the walls, and the completion of the Renovation the roof.”

Note: “In this comparison” explains the analogy; it is not an additional Middle Persian clause.
