## Fifteen translation and language findings

1. **Negative imperative versus statement.** `ma` with imperatives in103000044–047,052,056 and550000001–002 supports prohibitions; `nē` in conditional and declarative settings behaves differently. Preserve second-person singular in Adurbad and plural in550 instead of making every moral clause impersonal.
2. **Purpose/consequence and deontic modality need context.**103000071–081 has `bāš kū ... bawē`;138002007–017 and138007009 use `-išn`, `abāyēd`, `šāyēd`, `pādixšā`. A translator should separate “must,” “may/is permitted,” “can” and “so that.”138006004 `nē abāyēd` concerns non-necessity of yašt, not automatically prohibition.
3. **Enclitics and agency cross unit boundaries.** `u-š`, `u-t`, `kē-š` must be resolved against the active narrative participant, especially136 and151001131–135.134009022 has different hearer assignments in Farsi and English;215002001 offers alternative beneficiary relations. Do not silently choose an agent solely because the nearest modern translation is fluent.
4. **Ellipsis requires neighbouring units.**133000005–016/176–198,151032002–008 and the physiological comparison152030030 omit recoverable predicates. Explicit translator additions should remain distinct from text, as the note at152030029 requires for “is like.”
5. **Surface cognates are not a dictionary.** `afsōs`103000056 means mockery, not modern regret; `wistāx`133000181 concerns confidence; `xwadāy` can mean lord/master; `šahr` may be country; `bahr`139043003 covers ritual service/payment. `bōy`152030032 is consciousness but301006018/518000010 is fragrance in offering contexts.
6. **Explanatory ī and kū are part of Zand structure.**152027014 notes explanatory ī;301 repeatedly retains `{...}` commentary and `ast kē ... gōwēd` alternative exposition. Braces, square/angle brackets, asterisks and ordinary parentheses must survive structured export with their local meaning; none is safely a universal deletion marker.
7. **Textual variants can change the whole ontology or agency.**137001050 `asar kirb` versus `āsrō-kerpa` yields boundless body versus fire-form.151021006 `zamān` versus `yazdān` changes time to gods.152029006 supplies `dil`/Farsi heart but French has liver. These need variant records, not blended training labels.
8. **Manuscript-absent supplementation is explicitly identified.**134001006/008 add material from another chapter;151007004–005 and151062006–007 use Pazand. Attribution to a named printed edition alone does not mean the whole saved transcription reproduces that edition untouched.
9. **Competing historical rulings remain attributed.**138002079–082 gives different consequences for not retrieving remains from water when harm is possible;138002074 distinguishes agreement from disagreement. A single synthesized ruling loses the text's evidence.138006003–004 likewise disagrees over ritual qualification.
10. **Material connectivity drives technical meaning.**138002013–017,100–104 distinguishes joined and separate objects; translating `ē(w)-kardag` merely as “one” can erase the condition determining propagation of ritual pollution. Preserve geometry, dimensions and source units in77 rather than modernizing them without support.
11. **Names and historical geography need parallel aliases.**122/123 Avestanized English names should link to the source forms, not replace them.123000028 gives twenty versus twenty-one;123000018/041 Persian versus Parthian.205 alternatives can change a personal name into a material phrase. Dates and counts are especially unsafe to infer.
12. **Apparent translation defects are review candidates, not silently corrected sources.**133000015's Farsi order reverses bad speech/action labels;138005007 adds a second negative where the transcription and English are positive;138007004 omits the Farsi tanāpuhl measure;151001085's note appears to discuss086;152030032's French repeats a clause. Keep original and flag a proposed correction separately.
13. **Language scope and religious register are separate axes.**136/152 additional translations are French.504/538 are labelled Middle Persian and Parthian, and538028–031 visibly differs in morphology/lexicon. In Manichaean texts, `ardāwān` and `niyōšāgān` are community roles; `Ohrmezd`, `Wahman`, and `Mihr` must not inherit all Zoroastrian doctrinal meanings.
14. **Formulaic evidence is valuable but not independent duplication.** Completion111000036/134009025, testimony208001001, repeated construction205 and liturgical301 preserve useful templates. Repeated notes or copied formulae should not be counted as multiple independent supports for a contested lexical reading.
15. **Lacunae and source truncations limit translation.**136005008/010 French ends mid-sentence inside saved data, unlike the one tool truncation repaired during reading.203002001,205006001,518000013/017/018,544000001 and550 retain genuine gaps/uncertain letters. No fluent completion should be presented as a recovered translation.

## Original provisional translation exercises

These short renderings were composed for this reading report from the supplied transcriptions. They are not new critical editions and are not independently validated translations.

- **103000044** `čand tuwān hād mardōmān pad saxwan ma āzār.` Farsi: «تا آنجا که می‌توانی، با سخن به مردم آزار نرسان.» English: “As far as you can, avoid hurting people with your words.” The degree phrase's omitted possessor is inferred from the imperative.
- **151031006–007** `ud ān ī dānēnd xūb ud pad-nigerišn kunēnd / ud mizd dādīhā xwāhēnd`. Farsi: «کاری را که بلدند، خوب و با دقت انجام دهند و دستمزدی عادلانه بخواهند.» English: “They should do the work they know well and carefully, and ask for fair pay.” The obligation comes from the section's governing statement of workers' duties.
- **152029011**, selected clause: `karg-ē ī ēk-srū dīd`. Farsi: «کرگدنی تک‌شاخ دید.» English: “He saw a one-horned rhinoceros.” The surrounding analogy compares representation of a rhinoceros with indirect presentation of spiritual reality; it is not evidence for an imaginary unicorn.
