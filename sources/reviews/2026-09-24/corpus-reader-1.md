## Fifteen grammar and translation findings

1. **Infinitives can prescribe action.** The injunctions in `126000001–126000011` and clauses with `abāyēd dāštan` should become natural obligations in Farsi/English, not a list of isolated dictionary infinitives. Recurrent `ān ... kē ...` gives a test or definition.
2. **Clitics require clause-level agency analysis.** Forms containing `-š` can indicate an agent, possessor, or other relation according to the construction. `134007039` and `136003018` expose incompatible subject/object assignments between translations. Do not map every `-š` directly to “he” or “him.”
3. **`tā` has contextual force.** In `116000049`, the Farsi takes the clause as purpose concerning livelihood, whereas Tarapore's English takes duration and life-giving. A single fixed gloss cannot settle this difference.
4. **`kū` does several discourse jobs.** It introduces speech, complement clauses, and explanation. The note in `137001025` specifically favors an explicative reading. Preserve the distinction between a narrator's statement and a character's quoted claim.
5. **Negation can reverse a rule.** `nē` and prohibitive constructions need explicit scope. In `138002094`, the K witness adds `nē`; the resulting permitted/not-permitted opposition is a textual variant, not an error to reconcile silently.
6. **Blessings and predictions are not ordinary present facts.** Wishes such as those ending `112000021` and restoration passages such as `134008006` need optative or future force when context warrants it. The same surface tense does not dictate one English tense everywhere.
7. **Voice and auxiliary constructions deserve separate attention.** `152018001` raises passive `šnāsīhād`; `152019003` has a note favoring active syntax despite the Farsi; `152035023` distinguishes rising from causing to rise. The `raftan/raft ēstād` constructions in `152020001–002` must be interpreted as constructions, not word-by-word combinations.
8. **`ī` and `ud` are not interchangeable.** The Yasna notes in `301002011`, `301004013`, and `301004014` change relational linkage into coordination. This changes how many recipients or attributes a ritual list contains. Preserve the editor's intervention.
9. **Comparatives and authority words depend on genre.** Bozorgmehr's comparative answers rank items within an implicit domain. Meanwhile `pādixšāy`/related forms concern authority or power in wisdom literature but often permission in legal discussion (`138002094`). Do not force a royal gloss into every legal occurrence.
10. **Distinct faculties need stable, qualified labels.** `tan`, `gyān`, `ruwān`, `bōy`, and `frawahr` in `152029001–003` and `152030044–051` cannot all become “soul.” A working glossary should preserve body, life/life-soul, soul, consciousness, and fravaši distinctions while allowing author-specific usage.
11. **Legal plurality is part of the meaning.** `138002088–089` attributes opposing directions of corpse removal to Abarg and Mēdōmāh. Ch.8 distinguishes confession/repentance from restitution and debt. Translate each authority and condition, rather than manufacturing a single harmonized rule.
12. **Language and commentary boundaries must survive translation.** `137001029` and `138008022` contain Avestan quotations; `152035057` includes an uncertain Avestan expression; `526000001–003` is explicitly mixed Middle Persian/Parthian. In record 301, braces contain interpretive Zand and alternatives, not always a single syntactic continuation.
13. **Religious registers change lexical expectations.** Manichaean `ardāwān` refers to the Elect in its church context. Terms shared with Zoroastrian texts, including divine names, cannot automatically inherit the same referent or doctrine. Records 514, 517, and 529 provide contrasting narrative and institutional uses.
14. **Editorial reconstruction is not direct manuscript attestation.** Asterisks, brackets, ellipses, and explicit retroversion must remain visible. The note to `151000001` qualifies units 1–28 as reconstructed from Pazand; `151059007/009/010/013` also contain supplied Pazand expressions. These cannot be treated as unqualified witnesses to Pahlavi wording.
15. **Paragraph association does not certify translation alignment.** The repeated French paragraph in `152009004`, numeric conflicts, and Farsi commentary inside nominal “English translation” layers at `301007003/005` demonstrate why language and segment identity must be checked before reusing a pair.
