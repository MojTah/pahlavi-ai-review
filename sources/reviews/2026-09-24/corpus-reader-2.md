## Fifteen concrete translation and grammar findings

1. **Past transitive agreement is not automatically passive.** The explicit note at `parsig:152008001` explains plural frēstād hēnd through agreement with the sent demons; Ahreman is the agent. At `parsig:152024002`, -m in u-m ... mard-ē hāxt supports an agent-oriented “I brought one man to the faith,” while the principal Farsi describes the man joining him. Preserve both analyses and flag the agent issue.
2. **Clitics require discourse resolution.** u-š, u-m and ēg-it attach outside the main verb and can identify agent, recipient or possessor. `parsig:117000106` has the order to give a daily payment; `parsig:151001137` distinguishes the addressee who makes the speaker more honoured from the speaker's state. A token-by-token he/it mapping would fail.
3. **Negative imperatives and wishes have distinct morphology.** ma bāš / ma šaw in `151001098`, `152008004` are prohibitions; nē is not simply interchangeable. bawād and dahād in `217009001`, `222005001` express blessings, not past events. Manichaean āyād and related forms retain wish/invocation force.
4. **Obligation and possibility must remain separate.** abāyēd with an infinitive, šāyēd and tuwān, plus prescriptive -išn forms, contrast duty with feasibility: `136002004–008`, `138010025`, `151028004`. The ritual-law text's grammatical obligation is a historical assertion, not an instruction from the translator.
5. **ī is more than a possessive connector.** Relative/qualifying chains, genealogies and patronymics need different target structures. Compare `123000002` with the long `152007001` genealogy and the epitaphs in record 222. Do not force every ī to mean “son of”; the French genealogy supplies daughters in part of its chain.
6. **Comparatives need their comparison domain.** -tar often corresponds to a contextual superlative in questions, while kū can introduce a comparison: `133000061`, `151024004`, `151025007`. The rich/poor reversal in chapter 24 depends on keeping “among the wealthy” and “among the poor.”
7. **ka does not settle chronology or factuality.** It introduces future/iterative conditions and narrative time clauses. `151001095` uses repeated hād forms for a counterfactual about Kay Husraw. Rendering that as an actual failed resurrection would reverse the argument.
8. **bē/be needs local parsing.** It functions in verbal constructions and exclusion as well as directional expressions. `152004017` itself has a note resisting confusion of verbal be with a Persian preposition. “Except” in `116000015` and particle uses elsewhere cannot share one dictionary substitution.
9. **Parallel structures preserve argument, but numbers may conflict.** ēdōn ... čiyōn signals sustained analogy in `118000006–010` and `151043008–011`. `123000006` says seven settlements while giving eight names. `138001001` corrects written seven to eight on editorial grounds. Retain the discrepancy or apparatus instead of normalizing silently.
10. **Religious vocabulary is domain-specific.** kirbag, druwand, ahlaw, dēn, mēnōg, gētīg, xwarrah and tan ī pasēn require controlled but contextual glosses. Zoroastrian Ohrmazd and the Manichaean ohrmezd bay in `517000008` cannot be assumed identical referents merely because their forms resemble one another.
11. **Unit association is not sentence alignment.** `123000005` English begins material belonging to 006. `137001044–045` English moves a clause into the next unit. Many record-151 headings have no Farsi, and question/answer frames and list items are separately indexed. Build translation examples from inspected spans, not automatically from rows.
12. **Editorial signs carry evidence.** Stars, brackets, angle brackets, parentheses, ellipses and question marks distinguish proposed readings, supplied material and uncertainty. `134003029` has a supplied clause absent from the cited manuscripts; `151043011` is a Pazand-based addition. Removing these would erase the difference between witness and editor.
13. **Language classification must follow content.** The legacy EnTranslation source field contains French in records 117, 136 and 152 and Persian editorial notes in `138010025–029` and `528000006`. Records 538/542 explicitly cover Middle Persian and Parthian. `557000001/003` contain Turkic titles. Preserve these labels rather than manufacturing English translations or pretending every segment is Middle Persian.
14. **Technical and ordinary senses compete.** mēx in `152002021` is read as an astrological angle by Farsi but differently in French; sar marks a beginning/end disagreement in `134009009`; ēwēnag is mirror or “suchlike” in `138002005`. Lexical choice must be grounded in the specific edition and context.
15. **Genre changes the translation contract.** Law requires accurate scope/exception; genealogy requires identity preservation; epigraphy requires visible gaps; hymns require refrain and rubrics; polemic requires attribution. `538000038–042` retains tune names and signatures, whereas `542000004–009` needs explicit authorial attribution. A single prose-normalization policy would damage both.
