## Fifteen grounded language and translation findings

1. **Prohibition and purpose are distinct.** In `103000092`, *agar xwāhē kū … nē ašnawē … ma dah* nests a desired non-event under a command. In `132000003`, *ma dārēd kū-tān … nē āxēzēnd* contrasts the imperative prohibition with its intended consequence. Persian makes the link explicit; a blanket rule translating every *nē* as “do not” would fail.
2. **Wishes are not simple predictions.** *bawād* in `219001001` and `219004001` wishes the deceased a share in heaven; *anōšag bawēd* in `117000061` is courtly address. Compare the blessings at `132000079` and `150000056`. The plural/polite address, optative blessing and ordinary *bawēd* clauses need context.
3. **Clitics require syntactic analysis, not a fixed possessive gloss.** The note at `136002025` explicitly proposes past-transitive/ergative analysis with *tō* as agent for *burd/guft*. Notes at `152010008–009` flag *-š* alongside passives and consider different functions. These are competing analyses supplied by the corpus; a reader should not claim every *u-š* means “and he did.”
4. **Prescriptive verbal nouns and passives should remain distinct.** *be šōyišn*, *be kunišn*, *be nihišn* at `138002112–118` express what is to be done; `152010009` has explicit passive *abganīhist*, “was thrown.” The recurring impersonal instructions are not all infinitives or all imperatives.
5. **Conditional/perfect clauses carry the decisive timeline.** *ka … xward ēstēd* in `138002109–111` concerns ingestion before a later state/event. At `138002109` Persian describes a young animal born after a year, while Tavadia describes becoming pure a year after birth. At `138003006`, the Persian places prayer after changing/sitting, the English holds it until those actions. These are real sequencing differences, not stylistic paraphrases.
6. **Comparatives and ellipsis depend on their frame.** `151014004` explicitly compares rightful poverty with others' wealth using *weh kū*. `133000225–248` omits repeated copular/question material. `152022003–004` distinguishes good, better and best. Translating every comparative as an absolute superlative would obscure the argument.
7. **Discourse voice changes within a single record.** `509000004–006` moves from mission narration to *man/an Ammō* and then quoted dialogue; `134007019–020` relays divine instructions; `115000005–007` places contractual commitments inside reported acceptance. Person and speaker must be resolved before translating the verbs or assigning obligations.
8. **Language labels must survive extraction.** Records 117, 136 and 152 have French translations despite the original field name `EnTranslation`. `538000001–017` is explicitly Middle Persian and Parthian; `134005003` quotes Avestan prayers. `508000002` holds Persian commentary in an additional layer. None should be silently treated as homogeneous Middle Persian/English pairs.
9. **The marriage contract contains edition-level legal disagreement.** At `115000005`, the transcription's marked *ēkānagīh* and Persian “faithfulness” differ from Macuch's *ayōgānīh*, intermediary succession; *ērīh* is “modesty” in Persian but Iranian status in English. At `115000007–008`, Persian marriage/mahr framing differs from Macuch's property, income, substitute and substance analysis. Preserve both credited translations; do not manufacture consensus.
10. **Zand ī Wahman Yasn notes explain some, but not all, lexical differences.** `134004041` notes *ānāstag-dēn/xwāstag-dēn*; `134004061` notes *xwēdīh/hangadīh*; `134004067` discusses *xwah/xwān/sparān* and *halag/harg*. `134005002` has *nē* and a Persian negative comparison, against Cereti's “just as.” `134007036` reverses who sees whom. The latter differences merit edition checking rather than automatic correction.
11. **Numerical/calendar discrepancies are concrete audit targets.** `134004064` omits one-third in Persian, as its own note states. `137002019` has bracketed *aštād* versus Ard in both translations; `137002021` gives English day-number glosses inconsistent with the thirty-name list in `137002023`, whose Persian repeats Šahrewar. `152003030` has *šaš-sad* and French six hundred versus Persian six thousand; `152010015` has sixty versus French fifty parasangs. These observations concern displayed layers, not a new critical edition.
12. **Several propositions differ in polarity or participant relation.** `152001028` has capable/able in transcription, Persian and its note, against French “incapable”; `152001031` has separate-from versus with, explicitly discussed by the note. `116000025` Persian intangible thought/speech and tangible action differs markedly from Tarapore. `150000054` Persian loss of wealth contrasts with English “if it had not vanished.” Each needs a tagged disagreement, not silent smoothing.
13. **Unit boundaries are not sentence equivalence.** The victory/sealing phrase crosses `123000060–061`; the soul-versus-belly instruction crosses `132000009–010`; *pad frašgird hangām* shifts between `152034007–008` by stated editorial choice. `151014009–011` combines textual repair with a Persian 11 that appears to repeat/reassign the note's alternative for 9–10. Evaluate adjacent units together while retaining IDs.
14. **Editorial layers are part of the evidence.** `134007015` has an unfinished note ending in dots; `151026025` explicitly distinguishes 300, 600, and 600 years plus six months/sixteen days across witnesses; `151052004–005` marks/disputes Pazand supplements; `301007012–026` embeds Zand explanations in braces. Stars, restored words and lacunae in 209/219/501/517/540 cannot be promoted to secure manuscript readings.
15. **Shared vocabulary is not a shared doctrinal referent.** *xwadāy* means lord/ruler in courtly passages such as `117000069`, a divine addressee in hymns, and occurs in the explicitly Christian epitaph `223001001`. *Ohrmazd*, *Mihr*, *Āz* and *frawahr* occur in Zoroastrian and Manichaean cosmologies with different relations; `517000019` even glosses frawahr as ether. The body/social analogy at `152030018–020` and armament/virtue analogy at `151042007–013` likewise require genre-sensitive translation.

## Three short original provisional renderings

These are this reader's short renderings of supplied transcription, not quotations of a modern translator or manuscript-based corrections.

1. `103000092`, *agar xwāhē kū az kas dušnām nē ašnawē ō kas dušnām ma dah.*
   - فارسی: «اگر می‌خواهی از کسی دشنام نشنوی، به کسی دشنام نده.»
   - English: “If you wish to hear no insult from anyone, insult no one.”
   - The conditional scopes the purpose; *ma dah* is the command.
2. `151014004`, *driyōšīh ī pad frārōnīh weh kū tuwāngarīh ī az xīr ī kasān.*
   - فارسی: «تنگدستی با درستکاری از توانگری به مال دیگران بهتر است.»
   - English: “Poverty with upright conduct is better than wealth from other people's property.”
   - Provisional lexical choices: *frārōnīh* and *xīr* depend on the ethical/property context.
3. `152034006`, *…nūn ka būd, wišuft, abāz passāxtan hugartar.*
   - فارسی: «اکنون که بوده‌اند و از هم گسسته‌اند، دوباره فراهم‌کردنشان آسان‌تر است.»
   - English: “Now that they have existed and come apart, reassembling them is easier.”
   - The preceding wooden-container analogy motivates “reassembling”; the fragment alone does not identify every referent.
