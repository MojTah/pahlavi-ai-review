## Twelve grammar and translation findings

1. **Prohibitive ma and ordinary negation nē must stay distinct.** At 103000005, `tō-iz ... ma kun` addresses the hearer; 116000040's `ma burzēd` likewise prohibits exalting the wicked. Tarapore's “do not live” does not fit this supplied verb/parallel as readily as the Persian “do not exalt.” 110000006 contains plural prohibitions despite a declarative third-person English rendering.
2. **Clitics carry essential participants.** `u-m` in 116000001–000003 marks the speaker's relations and duties; `kū-t` in 151001024 marks the beneficiary/affected person. The note to 139041002 restores the omitted `u-š`. In 138002069–002070, whose hand supplies force determines who incurs which liability; dropping pronouns distorts the casuistry.
3. **The ī linker has several jobs.** It links possession/qualification in `uzwān ī rāst-gōwišnān` (133000206), and relative qualification in longer passages. An editorial change from ī to ud at 301003008 changes linkage into coordination. Treating all ī as an interchangeable Persian ezafe hides that decision.
4. **-išn can express prescription, not only an abstract noun.** `šōyišn` in 138002053 and 138002067 is rendered “must be washed”; `dārišn` at 151034012 is “should be regarded.” `abāyēd` plus infinitive also expresses obligation, e.g. 151015036. These are related but not identical constructions.
5. **Passive morphology protects agency.** `ōzanīhēd` at 152025010 is explicitly identified as passive in the note, whereas the main Persian translation makes Axt the killer. French separates his being killed from the deaths of six religious figures. `mālīhistan` at 152022013 also warns against inventing an explicit agent.
6. **Resultative/reportive formations need contextual tense.** `guft ēstēd` repeatedly introduces received teaching (111000001, 151001010); `mad ēstēd` in 151034007 is what has come to someone. At 152003011 the note correctly distinguishes supplied present `bawēd` from the Persian translation's past-looking “بود”. Do not mechanically call every participle-plus-copula clause a simple past.
7. **Exception scope changes rules.** The note at 138002029 interprets `bē ... tā` together as an exceptive construction, unlike a separate “then” for tā. Similar patterns recur at 138002068 and 138002071. Preserve the exception before simplifying the sentence.
8. **Comparatives are productive but sometimes pragmatically maximal.** `-tar` structures the entire 133000199–223 Q/A series and 117's royal choices. Meanwhile 151033004's `kem` limits evil's ability: less vulnerable is the supplied claim, not invulnerable. Do not strengthen its modality.
9. **Zand number agreement reflects its translated register.** The note at 301002016 explicitly explains plural `ōyšān` with uninflected nouns through the Avestan source. That example should not be generalized to every Middle Persian genre without further evidence. Braces at 301003005 mark explanation, not a continuous unmarked translation of the liturgical base.
10. **Lexical senses vary by domain.** `gōspand` in 152023002 covers five broad animal classes, not sheep alone. `anēr` in 139041001 receives a religious rather than simply ethnic gloss in the commentary. `puhl` must be interpreted contextually: 116000036 has bridge/expiation disagreement, whereas 138002020 is plainly a constructed bridge; the spelling is `puhl` in both supplied transcriptions.
11. **Body/soul vocabulary does not map one-to-one to modern psychology.** 110000003 distinguishes ōš, wīr, bōy, gyān, tan, kālbod; 152030036 assigns different functions to wīr, ōš, xrad. French there gives intelligence/memory/wisdom. Reusing a single English gloss mechanically would erase the local schema.
12. **Register and morphology differ across these Middle Persian materials.** The late batch's Manichaean forms include `dānistom` (548000002), `ašmāh`, `amāh`, `xwarxšēd`, hymnic `āyād` and imperatives such as `padīrēd` (554000017). The imperative/benedictive voice should not be flattened into factual statements. Shared deity names do not establish identical theology across Zoroastrian and Manichaean texts.

## Short original provisional renderings

These are newly worded, cautious renderings of the supplied romanized text, informed by the accompanying translations. They are not independent manuscript readings or validated replacement editions.

1. **parsig:103000005** — `har čē pad tō nē nēk tō-iz pad any kas ma kun.`
   - فارسی: «هرچه برای خودت نیک نیست، تو هم در حق دیگری انجام مده.»
   - English: “Whatever is not good for you, do not do to someone else either.”
   - `-iz` supplies “also/either”; the missing copula is supplied in translation.
2. **parsig:133000203–133000204** — `čē sūdōmandtar? nišastan abāg dānāgan.`
   - فارسی: «چه سودمندتر است؟ با دانایان نشست‌وبرخاست کردن.»
   - English: “What is more beneficial? Keeping company with the wise.”
   - “Keeping company” renders literal “sitting with” idiomatically without adding a new claim.
3. **parsig:216001001** — commissioning clause and final wish.
   - فارسی: «پورگ، پسر روشن‌آدر، فرمان داد این دخمه را برای روانش بسازند؛ بهشت بهره‌اش باد.»
   - English: “Pūrag, son of Rōšn-Ādur, commissioned this tomb for his soul. May paradise be his portion.”
   - The finite past `framūd` governs the infinitive `kardan`: ordered or commissioned something to be made. It is not an imperative form. The separate `bawād` expresses the wish; name vowels remain provisional. Lead rechecked this distinction against saved unit `216001001`.
