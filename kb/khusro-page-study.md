## Twelve supported additions and checks

The first six are source/interpretation controls with immediate consequences for a dictionary or translator. The later six are concrete language checks. Statements labeled **study inference** are my source-assisted analyses, not new claims attributed to the editor.

| # | Finding and consequence | Exact locator |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Text status changes inside one file.** The extant edited Pahlavi transcription occupies pp. 7–21. The Arabic version starts p. 22 and ends at the top of p. 26. The following Pahlavi section is expressly based on the Arabic version. Treat pp. 26–30 as a secondary rendering/reconstruction until its status is independently established, not as another ancient manuscript witness or independent training target. | pp. 7, 21–22, **26 heading**, 30; p. 26 visually checked |
| 2 | **Persian is mediated through Arabic.** The introduction describes the Persian version as based on the Arabic version. Its four pages are genuine source material, but pairing their successive sentences mechanically with the extant Pahlavi would create false alignments. For example, the Persian opening follows the setting under Parvēz. | p. 3 and pp. **31–34**; Persian pages visually read |
| 3 | **The story's royal identity differs by version.** The Pahlavi frame concerns Xusrō I, son of Kavād; the Arabic version places the story under Xusrō II/Parvēz. Store version identity with proper names. Do not silently make the king identical across corresponding-looking passages. | p. **3**; Pahlavi title p. 7; Arabic p. 22; Persian p. 31 |
| 4 | **Name versus epithet needs contextual annotation.** The introduction treats Vāspuhr as the page's name in the extant Pahlavi while explaining its princely lexical sense. It distinguishes the Arabic personal-name use of Xvašārzōg from the Pahlavi attributive use. A dictionary should preserve both lexical interpretation and name function. | p. **4**; extant §§1, 19 and final blessing, pp. 7, 9, 21 |
| 5 | **Questions do not map one-for-one across versions.** The editor lists thirteen principal Pahlavi topic groups against nineteen Arabic topics, with additions and a different order. Wine and shell-fruit questions, for example, occupy different ordinal positions. Use topic/section correspondence rather than page or sentence sequence for alignment. | Complete comparative list pp. **5–6**; extant §§43–60, pp. 12–13; Arabic pp. 23–25 |
| 6 | **An editorial historical claim stays attributed.** Asha regards the lion-catching and sexual-restraint episode as a scribal annex to an abridged treatise. This is the editor's argument, not a demonstrated date for every word in §§107–124. Mark the episode's proposed textual layer rather than excluding it automatically as non-Pahlavi. | p. **6**; extant §§107–124, pp. 19–20 |
| 7 | **Clitic role must be resolved in the clause.** In the opening, the third-person clitic accompanies the page's act of praising; later the same spelling accompanies the king's judgment. In §6 a first-person clitic occurs with the expression for the page's father. **Study inference:** these provide tests for changing referents and possessive versus agent interpretation, rather than treating every clitic as a fixed subject. | §§2–3 and **6**, p. 7; recurring approval formula beginning **§22**, p. 10 |
| 8 | **Agreement readings can be editorial decisions.** In §116 the edited text prints plural auxiliaries for the sins and good deeds but the apparatus records other readings. **Study inference:** this is a useful past-transitive/object-agreement exercise only if the editorial substitution remains visible. It must not be presented as unambiguous manuscript evidence for the paradigm. | **§116**, pp. 19–20, notes **68–69**; p. 19 visually checked |
| 9 | **A phrase may express death, not physical travel.** The note on the mother's departure compares the expression with inscriptions and gives its death-related sense. Record the complete idiom **ped baxt šud** with its context, rather than translating its parts as a trip to a place. | **§17**, p. 9, note **23** |
| 10 | **Food terms are especially unsafe modern look-alikes.** The note on **xāmiz** describes a preparation involving pickled/raw meat. Another explains **mayxvaš** as a sweet-and-sour/bitter-sweet quality of fruit. These senses require culinary context; spelling resemblance to modern words or the independent word for wine cannot determine the translation. | **§§33–35**, p. 11, note **34**; **§41**, p. 12, note **40** |
| 11 | **Broad entertainment vocabulary must not collapse to music.** The entertainment list includes string/wind performers, rope and chain performers, and other physical acts. **Study inference:** a broad translation such as entertainment is appropriate for the overall category, with narrower senses for named acts. A music-only glossary would lose the list's structure. | **§§60–63**, pp. 13–14; topic heading p. 5 |
| 12 | **Comparison formulas have scope.** Repeated lists first approve several items and then single out one as having no equal. **Study inference:** translating the repeated comparison formula as a generic denial of conflict would miss the preference ranking; likewise, a comparative ending alone does not identify which item is best without the following exception/comparison clause. | **§§24–26**, p. 10; **§§29–31**, pp. 10–11; **§§49–53**, pp. 12–13 |
