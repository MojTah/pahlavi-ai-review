## What the passage does

After the invocation, five statements repeat a judgment followed by a relative clause naming something the subject lacks: wisdom, a wife, offspring, wealth and someone to support them. A sixth statement ranks concern for the soul above the preceding matters; the final unit closes the text. These valuations belong to the historical counsel. They are not our judgments of the worth of people without spouses, children or wealth.

The recurring pattern is **predicate + ast + kē + noun + nē dārēd**. In these clauses `kē` introduces the person being described, and `nē` negates possession. Preserving that structure helps distinguish the speaker's judgment from the condition attached to it. The repeated third-person form does not justify mechanically substituting the same target verb in every later context.

## The important ambiguity in §6

The source transcription uses `ruwān nē dārēd`. The displayed Farsi first follows the literal possession wording, then adds a parenthetical explanation about failing to care for the soul. Keep the interpretive explanation separate from the literal surface: this paragraph alone should not redefine `ruwān` or assert a general dictionary rule that the verb always means care for.

**Our contextual Farsi study rendering:** از همهٔ اینان بدتر، کسی است که از روان خود غافل بماند.

**Our contextual English study rendering:** Worse than all these is someone who neglects their soul.

These natural renderings follow the site's parenthetical interpretation. They are not claimed as the only literal translations. A literal possession reading and the contextual interpretation remain separately recorded for later comparison.
