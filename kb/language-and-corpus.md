# Language and corpus

## Working scope

This project studies **Middle Persian (Pahlavi)**. Old Persian is the earlier Achaemenid language stage; modern Farsi is a New Persian variety. These stages are historically related, but require separate language labels and grammatical analysis. Pahlavi is not simply modern Farsi written in another alphabet. [S3](sources.md#s3), printed pp.7, 19–20.

“Pahlavi” here means Middle Persian as represented especially in the Zoroastrian literary tradition. Terminology varies historically: a reference to Pahlavi in an early source may concern Parthian or related northwestern speech. Do not identify a language from that name alone. [Early New Persian, historical introduction](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/persian-language-1-early-new-persian/)

Middle Persian belongs to the southwestern Iranian history of Persian and became an official language of the Sasanian state. Evidence includes inscriptions, coins, seals, documents, and literary manuscripts. Avestan is a different Iranian language; a Middle Persian translation or commentary on an Avestan text is not itself Avestan. [Iranian languages: documentation](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iran-vi2-documentation/)

## Keep the traditions distinct

| Tradition | What to record before reading |
|---|---|
| Inscriptional Middle Persian | Monument or object, edition, date, and whether a parallel Parthian version exists |
| Zoroastrian Book Pahlavi | Work, chapter, manuscript witness, and editor's reconstruction |
| Manichaean Middle Persian | Language identification as well as script; Manichaean script also represents other languages |
| Christian Middle Persian | Psalter passage, Syriac relationship, and distinction between composition and manuscript dates |
| Pāzand | Middle Persian represented in Avestan script; check the transmission and normalisation of the particular text |

This is a reading checklist, not a chronological classification of every surviving witness. Script background: [Manichaean script](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manichean-script/), [Pahlavi Psalter](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/pahlavi-psalter/), [Book Pahlavi proposal, pp. 8–9](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2014/14077-book-pahlavi.pdf).

Manichaean script also represents Parthian, Sogdian and Early New Persian, among other languages. Language can change within a witness or record. A Pāzand witness likewise supplies a transmitted reading that can contain later Persian adaptations; its vowels are comparative evidence, not a universal decoding key. [Manichaean script, description](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manichean-script/); [S3](sources.md#s3), printed p.19 n.9.

## Literary orientation

Many surviving Zoroastrian compilations reached their recorded form in the ninth and tenth centuries while preserving earlier material. Distinguish the age of an idea, composition, compilation, surviving copy, and modern edition. The corpus has a strong religious bias; it is not a representative sample of everyday conversation.

- **Kārnāmag ī Ardaxšīr ī Pābagān:** narrative about Ardashir; useful for following participants and events.
- **Bundahišn:** cosmology and creation; Iranian and Indian versions require separate identification.
- **Dēnkard:** a large compilation with varied subject matter.
- **Zand:** translations and explanations associated with the Avesta; translation language may differ from independent prose.

Background: [Cereti, Pahlavi literature](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/middle-persian/pahlavi-literature/). These are corpus landmarks, not a list of works read in full during this study.

## Religious vocabulary needs context

**Gētīg** concerns tangible or material existence; **mēnōg** concerns the spiritual or intangible. These are not reliable substitutes for “evil” and “good.” Their relation varies with the passage, including creation and eschatological contexts. A simple two-column vocabulary gloss cannot carry the entire theology. [Shaked, Gētīg and Mēnōg](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/getig-and-menog/)
