# Worked readings

These are **source-assisted analyses of published transcriptions**, not independent decipherments of manuscripts. English renderings below are my own. The source quotations are intentionally short.

## 1. An introduction to reported content

**ēdōn nibišt ēstād kū**

Reading: “it was written thus, that …” The writing participle and standing auxiliary form a verbal unit; the final word introduces the reported content. [TITUS Kārnāmag, I.1](https://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/iran/miran/mpers/kap/kap002.htm)

Check: recognise the auxiliary construction before attempting a word-for-word English rendering.

## 2. A prohibition with an enclitic

**kū-m wizand ud zyān mā kun**

Reading: “Do me no injury or harm.” The enclitic refers to the affected first person; the prohibition governs the imperative. [TITUS Kārnāmag, I.15](https://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/iran/miran/mpers/kap/kap002.htm)

The parallel request occurs in [Sanjana's 1896 translation, chapter 1, paragraph 15](https://www.avesta.org/pahlavi/karname.htm). That comparison supports the overall sense; it does not independently settle the grammatical labels.

## 3. Following the agent

**u-š pas duxt ī xwēš pad zanīh dād**

Reading: “Then he gave his own daughter in marriage.” Context identifies the giver as Pābag. Analyse the enclitic as the agent of the past transitive verb, with the daughter as object. [TITUS Kārnāmag, I.20](https://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/iran/miran/mpers/kap/kap002.htm)

[Sanjana, chapter 1, paragraph 20](https://www.avesta.org/pahlavi/karname.htm), supplies a useful narrative comparison. It is an older translation, not the final authority on every reading.

## 4. Written form and linguistic reading

**nzdst| ʾsmʾn| YHBWN-t|** → **nazdist asmān dād**

Reading: “First [he] created the sky.” Here a heterographic verb plus complement is read as a Persian past form. The English subject is supplied from context rather than from a separately quoted subject word. [University of Texas, Bundahišn excerpt](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/history-of-the-language/middle-persian/)

The apostrophe-like signs are normalised here to `ʾ`; consult the source for its typography. This three-word reading is not a fresh collation of the Bundahišn manuscript.

## What these checks establish

The examples exercise auxiliary recognition, prohibition, enclitic attachment, agent tracking, and the difference between spelling and reading. They are worked examples with visible sources, not a blind examination or a numerical proficiency score.

A narrative about a ruler also needs genre awareness. The Kārnāmag contains legendary material; translating a claim does not verify it as history. [Iranica: Kār-nāmag](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/karnamag-i-ardasir/)
