# Grammar for initial reading

These are recognition aids. Check the tradition and edition before applying a paradigm.

## Nouns and case

Middle Persian substantially reduced Old Iranian nominal inflection. Earlier material retains direct/oblique distinctions, especially in certain pronouns, kinship terms, and plurals. A general statement that Middle Persian “has no cases” loses this historical variation. Case **form**, syntactic **function**, and semantic **role** are different things. [Windfuhr, Cases, discussion of Middle Persian](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cases/)

Recognise **-ān** as a plural ending in later material, and **-īhā** as another attested plural formation. Their distribution has a history; do not assign them solely by modern Persian rules. [Windfuhr, West Iranian section](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cases/)

In the Zoroastrian corpus, plural marking is not obligatory; lack of a plural ending does not establish singular meaning. Ordinary nominal morphology lacks grammatical gender, with exceptional forms and annotation conventions requiring care. [UD Middle Persian, Nominal Features](https://universaldependencies.org/pal/index.html)

## Personal pronouns

| Person | Independent form for recognition | Enclitic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 singular | man; earlier an | -m |
| 2 singular | tō | -t |
| 3 singular | ōy | -š |
| 1 plural | amāh | -mān |
| 2 plural | ašmāh | -tān |
| 3 plural | awēšān | -šān |

This simplified inventory does not include every historical variant. The independent/enclitic distinction matters more than a modern Persian resemblance. [ASPIRANTUM, grammatical overview](https://aspirantum.com/curriculum/middle-persian-pahlavi-syllabus/introduction-to-middle-persian-grammar-and-history)

An enclitic can express possession, an object, or a past-transitive agent. Determine its role from the construction. [Windfuhr, Pronominal suffixes](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cases/)

Clitics commonly attach near the beginning of a clause. Their written host may be a conjunction, while their grammatical dependency lies elsewhere. An independent noun phrase and a clitic may also refer to the same participant; do not count them as two agents. [S3](sources.md#s3), printed p.22 n.15; [S24](sources.md#s24), printed pp.279, 282, §§3.2, 7.3–7.4.

## Linking and dependent phrases

The connective **ī** links a head to a dependent expression and is historically related to the later Persian ezāfe. Treat it as a grammatical relationship, not invariably as English “of.” [Sadeghi and Perry, Eżāfa](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ezafa/)

For a new phrase, mark the head, identify what depends on it, then decide whether English needs possession, an adjective, or another construction. Do not supply the same English preposition for every occurrence.

The dependent expression may be a noun phrase, adjective, prepositional phrase or clause. Relative clauses with **ī, kē, čē** require contextual analysis; an English who/what distinction is not an automatic decoder. A pronoun inside a relative clause can resume its head without introducing another participant. [S3](sources.md#s3), printed p.23, §2.10.2; [UD, coordination and subordination](https://universaldependencies.org/pal/index.html).

Subjects may be omitted, and objects and verbs tend towards the end of a clause. Use this as a reading expectation, not an inflexible word-order rule. [UD Middle Persian, Syntax](https://universaldependencies.org/pal/index.html)

## Verb recognition

Present and past stems must be learned separately. [ASPIRANTUM](https://aspirantum.com/curriculum/middle-persian-pahlavi-syllabus/introduction-to-middle-persian-grammar-and-history)

The following frequent indicative endings are a **recognition subset**, not a uniform paradigm for every corpus:

| Person | Ending |
|---|---|
| 1 singular | -ēm; -am/-om variants |
| 2 singular | -ēh / -ē |
| 3 singular | -ēd |
| 1 plural | -ēm / -om |
| 2 plural | -ēd |
| 3 plural | -ēnd |

Endings can leave person, number or mood unresolved. In the Manichaean paradigm of Oxford table 2.3, **-ād** marks both third-singular and second-plural subjunctive; **-ēd** has indicative and imperative uses. Later Zoroastrian material has a more restricted subjunctive inventory. The copula has **ast** in the third singular, with other forms based on **h-**. [Maggi and Orsatti, p.24, table 2.3 and n.16](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191056413_A35505953/preview-9780191056413_A35505953.pdf)

Past expressions can combine a past participle with an auxiliary, but the ordinary third-singular preterite omits the auxiliary: bare **šud** can be finite. Oxford distinguishes **šud hēm** (preterite), **šud būd hēm** (past preterite), **šud ēstēm** (perfect), and **šud ēstād hēm** (pluperfect). These morphological labels do not prescribe one English tense in every context. [S3](sources.md#s3), printed p.25, table 2.4; [S24](sources.md#s24), printed pp.282–283.

A form shaped like a past stem can instead be participial, a short infinitive or a converb. Test its relation to a modal or main verb before assigning tense and agency. This distinction is supported by the author's summary; the full monograph was not read. [Jügel, English Summary](https://www.geschkult.fu-berlin.de/e/iranistik/publikationen/iranica/iranica21/iranica-21-english-summary.pdf), printed pp.XXIX–XXX, referring to §§4.2–4.3.

## Past transitive clauses

Distinguish three participants: **S**, the sole participant of an intransitive clause; **A**, the agent-like participant of a transitive clause; and **O**, its object. Ergative alignment groups S with O in relevant grammatical behaviour. In Middle Persian, past-stem transitive constructions can show agreement with the object while the agent is expressed separately. Therefore, the agreement ending alone may not tell you who performed the action. [Payne, Ergative Construction](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ergative-construction/)

The historical explanation through participial constructions does not require translating every such clause as an English passive. Analyse the participants and discourse first. [UD Middle Persian, Diathesis](https://universaldependencies.org/pal/index.html)

Past-stem alignment must be distinguished from past-time reference: a modal or non-past interpretation does not by itself change the construction. Zero agreement also does not establish a third-singular agent. [Jügel, English Summary](https://www.geschkult.fu-berlin.de/e/iranistik/publikationen/iranica/iranica21/iranica-21-english-summary.pdf), printed pp.XXVIII, XXX–XXXI.

A collected example makes this consequence explicit: Parsig **152008001**, Zādspram 8.1, has **frēstād hēnd** with Ahreman as the sender and multiple demons as the sent participants. The database note advocates an active rendering and explains the plural agreement with the object, preserving a disagreement with the cited earlier Farsi interpretation. The French layer also presents the sending as active. This is a source-assisted example of argument analysis, not a new manuscript correction. [Saved source endpoint](https://mpdb.parsigdatabase.com/surf/paragraph/152/152008/All), editions credited in the unit: Anklesaria 1964 p.54; Gignoux and Tafazzoli 1993 p.63; Rashed-Mohassel 1385 SH p.56.

## Small words with large effects

- **nē** negates; **ma** is prohibitive. Editions may write the latter **mā**.
- **=iz** adds emphasis or inclusion; do not attach it to the wrong lexical stem.
- **be** and **hamē** have grammatical uses; a one-word English replacement is unsafe.
- **rāy** commonly marks beneficiary, purpose or cause; some late passages also use it to mark an object. Decide from the period and construction rather than mechanically importing modern **rā**. [S3](sources.md#s3), printed p.21, example 11; [S24](sources.md#s24), printed pp.282–283, §7.5 (in Nyberg's **rād** notation).
- **nēst** negates existence or copular predication; **ēw/hēb** can introduce an exhortation. A present-form verb can refer to the future. Preserve the clause's assertion, command, wish, condition or obligation. [S4](sources.md#s4), polarity/tags; [S3](sources.md#s3), printed p.24, §2.10.3.

Scholarly analyses of object-marking **ō** differ: Oxford p.21 allows it more broadly, while Jügel's summary p.XXVIII rejects most such analyses. Keep the disagreement visible; **ō** is not an unconditional direct-object marker.

## Checks from the supplied manuals

The following are source-assisted recognition rules from the complete grammar sketches studied in [S22](sources.md#s22), printed 63–85, and [S24](sources.md#s24), printed 275–284. They do not replace a corpus-specific analysis.

### Agreement does not identify the agent by itself

The primer's teaching examples distinguish past transitive and intransitive constructions. Our target-language translations below are study renderings of those examples, not a newly edited manuscript passage. [S22, printed 76–78 / PDF 79–81]

| Example | Participants and agreement | Farsi | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| man (tō) dīd hē | man = agent; tō = object; hē agrees with the second-person object | من تو را دیدم. | I saw you. |
| man mardān dīd hēnd | man = agent; mardān = plural object; hēnd agrees with the object | من مردان را دیدم. | I saw the men. |
| raft hēm | Intransitive: hēm agrees with the person who went | رفتم. | I went. |

Parentheses around tō follow the primer's presentation. A literal historical paraphrase involving a participle can help explain the construction, but does not require an English passive. Nyberg's grammatical terminology must be read with this distinction in mind. [S24, printed 282]

### Recognise forms, then test the construction

| Form or pattern | Recognition aid | Check before translating | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| -īhā | Plural formation or adverb formation | Is this a noun phrase or a modifier of an action/property? | S22, printed 67, 70 |
| xwad; xwēš | Reflexive and possessive uses respectively | Do not treat the two as interchangeable modern synonyms | S22, printed 69 |
| Y/ZY → ī; MNW → kē; MH → čē | Relative/linking expressions in the stated convention | Separate the connective from an attached clitic; compare documentary ī-š | S22, printed 69; S23, printed 21–22 |
| -ēn- | Causative/denominative stem formation | Identify the actual stem and its attested meaning | S22, printed 80–82 |
| -īh- | Intransitivizing derivation; passive/middle interpretation depends on base and context | Identify the stem and construction; distinguish the separately described -s- inchoatives | S22, printed 81–82; S3, printed 25; S24, printed 282 |
| -išn | Verbal noun; can express necessity | Preserve required or appropriate action when the construction demands it | S22, printed 83; S24, printed 281, §5.8 |

For prepositional phrases, identify the complete relation: az, pad and ō, postposed rāy, and combinations with clitics need clause-level analysis. Modal, subjunctive and prohibitive forms also need separate treatment; a smooth indicative translation can erase obligation, possibility or prohibition. [S22, printed 71–78]
