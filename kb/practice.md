# Practice and self-check

Original exercises based on this knowledge base. They are intended for a later retrieval attempt, not evidence that an independent test has already been passed. Use the named pages after attempting an answer.

## Questions

1. In the [heterogram table](writing-system.md), choose one entry and distinguish its written form, linguistic reading, and English meaning.
2. Why does an alphabet chart alone not establish a Book Pahlavi word's pronunciation?
3. In the [pronoun table](grammar.md), identify the second-person plural independent and enclitic forms. What remains unresolved without a sentence?
4. A past transitive verb has a plural agreement form. Why can you not conclude that several people performed the action?
5. In [worked reading 1](worked-readings.md), which two elements need to be interpreted together?
6. In worked reading 2, distinguish the imperative's addressee from the person referred to by the enclitic.
7. In worked reading 3, explain what identifies the giver. Is the English rendering alone enough evidence?
8. In worked reading 4, why are square brackets appropriate around the English subject?
9. An old grammar and a newer corpus use different labels for the same construction. What checks should precede choosing a label?
10. A glossary lists a heterogram that you cannot find in running text. What can you safely claim?
11. A familiar religious term appears in a different genre. What would make a one-word translation misleading?
12. What additional evidence is required before changing “I analysed a transcription” to “I deciphered the manuscript”?

## Answer guide

1. Keep the script-level representation separate from the proposed spoken word and its contextual gloss; consult the cited entry.
2. The [writing-system page](writing-system.md) identifies several independent sources of ambiguity. Explain at least three.
3. Use the table, then name the missing syntactic role and referent. A form alone is not a complete analysis.
4. Work through S, A, and O in the [ergativity section](grammar.md). State which participant controls agreement in the construction under examination.
5. Identify the participle and auxiliary, then give a structural account of the clause.
6. The person receiving the command and the affected first person are different participants. Preserve that distinction in translation.
7. Use the preceding narrative and the clause's grammatical structure. The English sentence is the result to justify.
8. The short quoted segment does not contain a separate subject word. The translation makes the contextual supplement visible.
9. Compare tradition, date, witness, definition, and analytical aim. Check whether the disagreement affects translation or only terminology.
10. Claim occurrence in that glossary with its exact locator. Running-text attestation remains unverified.
11. Explain what the passage says, not only what the word can mean elsewhere. Consult the discussion linked from [Language and corpus](language-and-corpus.md).
12. Inspect the actual witness or reliable facsimile and document the sign reading, segmentation, and comparison with an edition.

Record mistakes, corrections, and the evidence that resolved them. Do not convert this answer guide into a fluency score.
