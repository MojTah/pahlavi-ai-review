## Why the revision in §11 matters

The page records an earlier interpretation with a negative element and a comparison about misfortune. The current transcription instead contains assistance and deliverance. The note attributes the earlier approach to Mahyar Navabi and Aryan, and the change to a suggestion by **میثم محمدی**. These are credits reported by the site; the underlying publications have not been independently checked here.

Retain two source-version records with a status such as `current` and `superseded_on_site`. Do not pool them as equivalent target translations. In particular, a system that discards the changed negation could produce the opposite religious claim while appearing fluent. The absence of the previous negative in the current reading is part of the editorial evidence, not a normalization choice.

## Source-assisted reading checks

These were worked with the source visible. They assess the consistency of the notes, not unseen translation skill.

1. **Does §1 compare amounts of money only?** No. The paired comparatives connect being richer with being more content. An English rendering must preserve that relationship.
2. **Is §2 a generic definition of giving?** No. `stānēm` and `dahēm` are coordinated first-person forms in the hand-to-hand example. The surviving toil belongs to the speaker.
3. **Should every `xwardan` be translated “eat”?** No. With `sōgand`, the expression concerns swearing an oath. The anger expression is additionally marked doubtful in §7, so its interpretation needs that qualifier.
4. **Can §11's earlier and current versions be used as two interchangeable reference answers?** No. Their polarity and predicate differ. Keep their editorial status explicit.
5. **Does a fluent version remove supplied verbs?** It may express the intended repeated verb in the target language, but the analysis must still record where the source supplied it in brackets.
