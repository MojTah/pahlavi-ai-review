# Method for a new passage

This is an original working procedure for this knowledge base. It makes a reading inspectable and keeps unresolved decisions visible.

## Before translating

Record the work, chapter or line, edition, page, and witness. State what is available: an image, printed Pahlavi, transliteration, or transcription. If only a transcription is available, do not claim to have read the script.

Identify the text tradition. Read the edition's notation key. Keep the original supplied text unchanged in the working record; make normalisation a separate field.

## Parse in a repeatable order

1. Find plausible clause boundaries. Do not treat modern punctuation as necessarily manuscript punctuation.
2. Identify the verb phrase and particles. Test finite, participial, infinitival and converb analyses, including a finite past with an omitted auxiliary.
3. Separate clitics from their hosts analytically while preserving the source spelling. Check whether a clitic resumes an already named participant.
4. Mark participants: who acts, what is affected, and what the verb agrees with.
5. Resolve dependent noun phrases and each connective.
6. Check unknown vocabulary in a dictionary and in comparable passages from the same tradition.
7. Produce a literal structural rendering, then readable English or Persian.
8. Recheck that the readable rendering preserves negation, modality, participants, and uncertainty.

## Record alternatives without guessing

| Field | What belongs here |
|---|---|
| Source locator | Work, witness, edition, chapter, line/page, URL |
| Written evidence | Exact reading or image pointer; preserve editorial marks |
| Proposed transcription | Linguistic reading and the convention followed |
| Analysis | Lemma, form, syntactic role, clitic attachment |
| Translation | Literal rendering and readable rendering |
| Alternatives | Competing readings and their consequences |
| Confidence | Strong / provisional / unresolved, with a reason |
| Next check | A specific dictionary entry, parallel, or manuscript detail |

“Strong” means supported in the stated context, not universally correct. “Provisional” means a plausible analysis lacks an important check. “Unresolved” means the evidence does not select a defensible reading.

## Quality checks

- Does every translated participant have a textual or clearly stated contextual basis?
- Did a clitic become a possessive simply because that was the first familiar interpretation?
- Was the past transitive construction checked before assigning the agent?
- Does the rendering accidentally add certainty, causality, intention, or a historical fact?
- Are supplied words and damaged or disputed readings visible?
- Can another reader find the same passage and understand the choice made?

When sources differ, compare their witnesses, conventions, dates, and aims. Two websites reproducing the same edition are not two independent witnesses. A dictionary definition is not a replacement for sentence analysis.

A filled translation field is not proof of a translation: the saved Parsig data includes attribution-only fields and uninterpreted fragments. Likewise, a modern reconstruction from Arabic is not an independent ancient Pahlavi witness. Apply these source checks before comparing target sentences. See the [short-text findings](short-texts-review.md) and [Xusrō edition study](khusro-page-study.md).

## Adding knowledge later

Add an entry only when it improves a real reading or answers a recurring question. Give it a source locator and label its scope. Preserve an earlier disputed reading as an alternative when useful; correct errors openly. Maintain the short index and the study status instead of accumulating repeated summaries.

For extended quotation or reuse of scans, check the source's rights. Keep downloaded originals separate from authored notes. Do not reproduce a modern edition wholesale as a new dictionary or reader.
